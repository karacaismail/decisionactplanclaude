- New primary source: MacIntosh, MacLean and Seidl studied 99 strategy workshops in 10 organizations (small and larger private firms and public bodies). One-off workshops were much less effective than a series. Only 3 of 10 cases met the participants' own success criteria, and each of those ran over at least 12 months. Success depended on the elapsed duration of the series, workshop frequency, the scope and autonomy of the unit, and participants' seniority. Only a minority of workshops involved middle managers. In one case, follow-up interviews found nothing had changed and actions were never followed through. The authors were also the facilitators, which they flag as a limit. ## 2. Published scored readiness checklists and academic readiness instruments

### Takeaway
No consultancy or strategy-software vendor publishes a scored, validated readiness test for strategic planning that I could find. Public checklists (Lord Cultural Resources, Bridgespan) are short yes/no lists without scoring. The best academic bases are ORIC (Shea et al. 2014; 12 items, two facets, organization-level) and Holt et al. (2007; four beliefs, individual-level). Both were built for a specific change, mostly in health or public settings, so adapting them to "strategic planning readiness" in a Turkish family SME needs fresh validation.

### Cited Findings

**ORIC (Organizational Readiness for Implementing Change)**
- Shea, Jacobs, Esserman, Bruce and Weiner, "Organizational readiness for implementing change: a psychometric assessment of a new measure", *Implementation Science* 9:7, 2014, open access (CC BY 2.0). [V1, full text] — [DOI 10.1186/1748-5908-9-7](https://doi.org/10.1186/1748-5908-9-7); [Europe PMC PMC3904699](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC3904699)
- ORIC measures two facets from Weiner's theory: change commitment and change efficacy. Example commitment item: "we are committed to implementing this change". Efficacy items are phrased "People who work here are confident that…". Raters used 12 items on a 5-point scale from "disagree" to "agree". [V1]
- Four studies: content adequacy test; exploratory and confirmatory factor analysis in lab vignette studies (CFI 0.98, TLI 0.97, SRMR 0.04, RMSEA 0.06 in the re-analysis); a test of aggregating individual answers to the organization level; and a small field study. Inter-rater reliability and agreement supported aggregation to the organization level. [V1]
- Limits stated by the authors: one commitment item was dropped by mistake in the field test; all field organizations showed high readiness, so the scale needs testing where readiness varies; predictive validity was not yet shown; the authors frame it for healthcare change efforts. [V1]

**Holt et al. (2007) readiness for organizational change scale**
- Holt, Armenakis, Feild and Harris, "Readiness for Organizational Change: The Systematic Development of a Scale", *Journal of Applied Behavioral Science* 43(2), 2007, pp. 232–255. Over 900 members of public and private organizations took part; the questionnaire was tested in two organizations. Readiness is multidimensional: change-specific efficacy, appropriateness, management support, and personal valence. It measures readiness at the individual level. [V2, Crossref abstract] — [DOI 10.1177/0021886306295295](https://doi.org/10.1177/0021886306295295)
- A review rates its method as strong and reliability as acceptable; the readiness factors explained some variance in job satisfaction and affective commitment, not turnover intentions. [V3, search extract of a registry entry] — [NCCMT registry entry](https://nccmt.ca/resources/search/226)

**Public practitioner checklists**
- Lord Cultural Resources, "Strategic Planning Readiness Checklist": five yes/no conditions: the director has been in post at least one year; knows the institution's culture, strengths and weaknesses; has the confidence of board and staff; director and board are committed; resources are available. Each has a "why this matters" note. No scoring. [V1] — [Lord checklist PDF](https://www.lord.ca/uploads/tools/Lord_Readiness-Checklist-Strategic-Planning.pdf)
- Bridgespan "New Strategy Checklist" (file metadata dated 23 July 2019): five questions: agreed impact; how impact will be achieved; specific priorities; resources needed; a measurable way to track progress. No scoring. [V1] — [Bridgespan PDF](https://www.bridgespan.org/getmedia/1d420b14-db22-49ec-88b8-7fa56a346192/kickstart-your-strategic-planning-process-checklist.pdf)
- Leadership Strategies publishes a 20-point assessment of an organization's strategic process; a SurveyMonkey "Strategic Planning Readiness Assessment" focuses on board readiness. Neither scoring method was seen. [V3, search extracts] — [Leadership Strategies](https://leadstrat.com/?p=4926); [SurveyMonkey form](https://www.surveymonkey.com/r/SPPREADASMT)
- Allison and Kaye's *Strategic Planning for Nonprofit Organizations* (Wiley; 3rd edition chapter 1 "Step 1: Set Up for Success") contains readiness questions, but the list was not readable. [V2, chapter title only] — [O'Reilly chapter page](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/strategic-planning-for/9781118768150/c01.xhtml)

### Inferences
- A product readiness score could borrow ORIC's two facets (commitment, efficacy) and Holt's four beliefs (efficacy, appropriateness, management support, personal valence), worded for "our strategic planning effort". Lord's and Bridgespan's items add leader tenure, board or owner commitment, resources, and agreed purpose. This blend is my synthesis, not a validated instrument.
- ORIC is organization-level and needs several respondents per firm. In a 50–499 employee family firm, it could be filled by the owner, family members and unit leads, then aggregated. Shea et al.'s aggregation checks would need repeating.
- Any score shown to a client must say it is not validated for strategic planning or for Turkey. This follows from the authors' own limits.

### Gaps
- No scored readiness instrument from McKinsey, BCG, Bain, Deloitte, OnStrategy, Balanced Scorecard Institute, ClearPoint, Cascade or similar vendors was found in public sources.
- Weiner (2009), "A theory of organizational readiness for change", *Implementation Science* 4:67, was not opened directly (my Europe PMC lookup returned a different paper). Its content is known here only via Shea et al.
- No Turkish translation or validation of ORIC or Holt's scale was searched for.
- Bryson's model readiness questionnaire was not seen.

## 3. Including front-line, shop-floor and young employees, and safe anonymous input

### Takeaway
Toyota's own media gives hard, current numbers on shop-floor suggestion systems, including a target for first- and second-year employees. Voice research shows that employees often stay silent out of fear or futility beliefs, and one 2024 experimental study found anonymity raised willingness to speak up through psychological safety. None of this research studies apprentices in strategy making. The evidence supports input from the shop floor, not decision rights.

### Cited Findings
- Toyota's Creative Idea Suggestion System has a history of more than 70 years and about 50 million suggestions in total. In 2023 about 810,000 suggestions were submitted, about 67,000 per month, or 14.4 per person, counting plant technical staff only. [V1, Toyota's own media, 31 October 2024] — [Toyota Times](https://toyotatimes.jp/en/series/Imaginative_and_creative/001_1.html)
- Process: the employee notes a problem, investigates, proposes and implements an improvement with supervisor support, checks results, submits a form, and the supervisor reviews it on site (genchi genbutsu) before a reward. Rewards run from 500 yen to 200,000 yen; awards of 50,000 yen or more are decided by a committee in each plant or division. A 2019 revision scores both outcomes and process. Most ideas are small improvements. [V1] — [Toyota Times](https://toyotatimes.jp/en/series/Imaginative_and_creative/001_1.html)
- A "Rookie Award" goes to first- and second-year employees who submit at least 36 suggestions a year, and new production employees get guidance toward this. Paper and digital submissions are split evenly, partly because production staff cannot freely use computers. 1980s peaks pushed quantity over quality, which later changed the system. [V1] — [Toyota Times](https://toyotatimes.jp/en/series/Imaginative_and_creative/001_1.html)
- Morrison (2014) defines upward voice as voluntarily sharing suggestions, concerns, problems or opinions with someone higher up; withholding them is silence and deprives the organization of useful information. The review covers antecedents and outcomes of voice and silence. [V2, abstract] — [Morrison, *Annual Review of Organizational Psychology and Organizational Behavior* 1, 2014, pp. 173–197](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-orgpsych-031413-091328)
- Detert and Edmondson, "Implicit Voice Theories: Taken-for-Granted Rules of Self-Censorship at Work", *Academy of Management Journal* 54(3), 2011, pp. 461–488. [V2, Crossref] — [DOI 10.5465/amj.2011.61967925](https://doi.org/10.5465/amj.2011.61967925). Four studies found that taken-for-granted beliefs about the risks of speaking up are widely held and explain workplace silence beyond established predictors, even for pro-organization suggestions. [V3, research database summary] — [Psych Safety database](https://explore.psychsafety.com/n/detert-edmondson-2011/)
- Detert and Burris, "Leadership Behavior and Employee Voice: Is the Door Really Open?", *Academy of Management Journal* 50(4), 2007, pp. 869–884. [V2, metadata only] — [DOI 10.5465/amj.2007.26279183](https://doi.org/10.5465/amj.2007.26279183)
- Liu, Yuan and Luo, "Technical Anonymity and Employees' Willingness to Speak Up", *Management Communication Quarterly* 39(2), 2025 issue (online 16 September 2024), pp. 322–358. Two experiments: employees were more willing to speak up under technical anonymity than without it, and psychological safety mediated this effect. Effects held under prohibitive voice solicitation and under time delay. The authors note many firms run anonymous online forums but whether they work is an open question. [V2, Crossref abstract] — [DOI 10.1177/08933189241284598](https://doi.org/10.1177/08933189241284598)
- Block: where fear, insecurity or mistrust prevails, people withhold or distort information, and diagnosis based on it fails. [V1] — [Flawless Consulting 4th ed. reading sample, p. 15](https://content.e-bookshelf.de/media/reading/L-19331124-8711f7bb5c.pdf)

### Inferences
- Toyota's model channels shop-floor ideas into small, supervised improvements with on-site review. For a shoe workshop, apprentices and journeymen could feed improvement ideas and problem reports, not vote on strategy. This is my mapping.
- The anonymity evidence is experimental and short-term. It supports offering an anonymous channel, but it does not show that anonymous input improves strategy quality.
- In a small workshop, an "anonymous" answer from the only apprentice in a unit is identifiable. Anonymity needs minimum group sizes before results are shown. This is my inference, and it links to KVKK's definition of anonymization in section 4.

- Detert and Burris (2007) findings were not read; only the citation is confirmed.

## 4. Turkish law on apprentices and minors, and what it means for interviewing or surveying them

### Takeaway
Apprentices (çırak) and candidate apprentices (aday çırak) have student status under Law 3308 and work under a written apprenticeship contract signed with their parent or guardian while they are minors. Under-18s are protected by Labour Law 4857 Articles 71–73 and the 2004 regulation on child and young workers: limited hours, 14 hours' continuous daily rest, no night work (20:00–06:00), no national-holiday work, and the employer must inform the parent about the job, its risks and measures. Employer-required meetings count as working time. KVKK has no special age rule for consent, so practice leans to parental consent plus the child's own agreement for under-18s, and to designing collection so that no personal data is processed at all. A lawyer must confirm each point.

### Cited Findings

**Law 3308 Mesleki Eğitim Kanunu (consolidated text on mevzuat.gov.tr, amendment table ending with Law 7346, in force 25 December 2021)** [all V1] — [3308 consolidated PDF](https://www.mevzuat.gov.tr/mevzuatmetin/1.5.3308.pdf)
- Art. 3(b): "aday çırak" is a person below the apprenticeship starting age who is introduced to the workplace and given basic knowledge of a trade before apprenticeship. Art. 3(c): "çırak" is a person who develops the knowledge, skills and work habits of a trade on the job under an apprenticeship contract. Art. 3(e): "kalfa" can do the trade's tasks to acceptable standards under a master's supervision.
- Footnote to the law: Law 6764 (2016) renamed "Çıraklık Eğitimi Merkezi" to "Mesleki Eğitim Merkezi" throughout. "MESEM" is the common abbreviation for these centres; the abbreviation itself does not appear in the law text.
- Art. 9: graduates of primary education (ilköğretim) may be trained as aday çırak in workplaces until the apprenticeship period.
- Art. 10 (as amended by Law 7180, 2019): to enrol as an apprentice student in a Mesleki Eğitim Merkezi programme, a person must have at least completed ortaokul or imam-hatip ortaokulu and be physically and medically fit for the trade. For dangerous or very dangerous trades, the Ministry sets education and age conditions. The amended article states no general minimum age.
- Art. 11: aday çırak and çırak have student status and student rights; they are not counted in the workplace's worker numbers.
- Art. 12: they get at least 8 hours a week of general and vocational education with paid leave; practical training happens at the workplace under a usta öğretici (master trainer).
- Art. 13: in covered provinces and trades, workplaces may not employ persons under 19 without an apprenticeship contract (graduates of formal vocational schools and holders of a kalfa certificate excepted). Before starting, the employer must sign a written apprenticeship contract with the apprentice's veli (parent) or vasi (guardian), or with the apprentice if adult.
- Art. 14: apprenticeship starts with a trial period of one to three months, which is paid.
- Art. 15: a workplace must have a usta öğretici to take apprentices.
- Art. 20: students in işletmelerde mesleki eğitim get at least 12 hours a week of theoretical education within working hours, counted as paid leave.
- Art. 21: these students must follow the workplace's conditions and working order.
- Art. 25: minimum pay for aday çırak and çırak is 30 percent of the age-appropriate minimum wage; for students in işletmelerde mesleki eğitim, 30 percent of the net minimum wage in workplaces with 20 or more staff and 15 percent below 20; 50 percent for Mesleki Eğitim Merkezi 12th-grade students who reached kalfa level. These payments are exempt from all taxes. Insurance premiums are calculated on 50 percent of the minimum wage.
- Art. 26: one month of paid leave each year during the holiday months.

**Law 4857 İş Kanunu (consolidated text)** [V1] — [4857 consolidated PDF](https://www.mevzuat.gov.tr/mevzuatmetin/1.5.4857.pdf)
- Art. 69: "night" in working life starts at 20:00 at the latest and lasts until 06:00 at the earliest, at most 11 hours.
- Art. 71 (as amended by Law 6645, 2015): employing children under 15 is banned. Children who are 14 and have completed compulsory primary education may do light work that does not harm their development or schooling. Under-14s may work only in art, culture and advertising with a written contract and a permit per activity. The work must not hinder school attendance or vocational education. Children who completed compulsory education and are not in formal education may work at most 7 hours a day and 35 a week; for those who turned 15, up to 8 and 40. Children attending school may work at most 2 hours a day and 10 a week during term, outside school hours.
- Art. 72: under-18 males (and women of any age) may not work underground or underwater.
- Art. 73: under-18s may not work at night in industrial work.

**Regulation on the Procedures and Principles for Employing Child and Young Workers (Resmî Gazete 6 April 2004, no. 25425; MevzuatNo 5457)** [V1, text read from a copy of the mevzuat.gov.tr page printed 19 December 2025] — [copy of the regulation](https://www.oyuncularsendikasi.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/COCUK-VE-GENC-ISCILERIN-CALISTIRILMA-USUL-VE-ESASLARI-HAKKINDA-YONETMELIK.pdf); [official mevzuat entry](https://mevzuat.gov.tr/anasayfa/MevzuatFihristDetayIframe?MevzuatTur=7&MevzuatNo=5457&MevzuatTertip=5)
- Art. 4: "genç işçi" (young worker) is a person aged 15 to under 18. "Çocuk işçi" (child worker) is a person who turned 14, is under 15 and finished primary education.
- Art. 5: under-18s may only do jobs listed in the annexes. Even in allowed jobs, they may not work on piece-rate or premium pay, in work needing continuous standing or close attention, or in the night period of 4857 Art. 69. Graduates of 3308 vocational schools aged 16 or more may work in their trade outside the annex limits if health, safety and morals are fully secured.
- Art. 5/A (added 2017): the Ministry of National Education decides which jobs and places are in scope for workplace training, internships and apprenticeship of vocational students.
- Art. 6: daily working time must respect 14 hours of continuous rest in each 24 hours. Breaks: 30 minutes for work over 2 and under 4 hours; 1 hour in the middle for 4 to 7.5 hours.
- Art. 7: working time includes time in training the employer must give, and time in courses and meetings outside the workplace to which the employer sends the worker.
- Art. 8: weekly rest of at least 40 continuous hours. Art. 9: no work on national and general holidays. Art. 10: at least 20 days of annual paid leave.
- Art. 12: the employer must inform the parent or guardian about the job, its risks and the measures taken, and must sign a written employment contract with the parent or guardian.
- Art. 13: the employer gives risk and induction training before work starts.

**Law 6698 KVKK (consolidated text, amendment table ending with Law 7499, in force 1 June 2024)** [V1] — [6698 consolidated PDF](https://www.mevzuat.gov.tr/mevzuatmetin/1.5.6698.pdf)
- Art. 3: "açık rıza" (explicit consent) is consent on a specific matter, based on information and given freely. "Anonim hâle getirme" means data can no longer be linked to an identified or identifiable person, even when matched with other data.
- Art. 4: processing must be lawful and fair, accurate, for specific, explicit and legitimate purposes, relevant, limited and proportionate, and kept only as long as needed.
- Art. 5: personal data may not be processed without explicit consent unless another listed condition applies, for example a contract, a legal obligation, or the controller's legitimate interest that does not harm the person's fundamental rights.
- Art. 6 (as amended 2024): special-category data (including health, religion, union membership, biometrics) may be processed only on the listed grounds and with Board-set safeguards.
- Art. 10: when collecting data, the controller must tell people its identity, the purpose, recipients, the collection method and legal basis, and their rights.
- Art. 28(1)(b): the law does not apply to processing that is anonymized through official statistics for research, planning and statistics.
- The law contains no age threshold for consent. My search of the text for "yaş", "çocuk" and "küçük" found no rule on minors. [V1, my reading]

### Inferences (practical rules for a strategy project; a lawyer must confirm each)
1. Timing. An interview, survey or workshop for an apprentice or other under-18 worker that the employer asks for is working time (Regulation Art. 7). It must fit within the daily and weekly limits, must not fall between 20:00 and 06:00, must not fall on a national or general holiday, and must not cut into school or MESEM theory days (3308 Arts. 12 and 20). Breaks under Regulation Art. 6 apply.
2. Parent information. The employer already must inform the parent about the job and its risks (Regulation Art. 12). Before personal data of an under-18 is collected, the safest route is a separate written information notice to the parent and to the young person, in plain words. Whether a separate parental consent is needed depends on the legal basis chosen. A lawyer must decide this.
3. Prefer no personal data. The cleanest design is a truly anonymous survey: no names, no worker numbers, no free text that identifies someone, no audio or video recording, and no small-group breakdowns. If data cannot be linked to a person even with other data, it is anonymous under KVKK Art. 3. In a small unit, age plus job title can identify the only apprentice, so such answers must be grouped with others before reporting.
4. If names or recordings are needed. Use a documented legal basis, tell people under Art. 10, keep data minimal and time-limited (Art. 4), and do not ask for consent if another basis applies (KVKK guide). If consent is used, keep the consent text separate from the information notice (decision 2026/347, V3). For under-18s, practice recommends both the parent's consent and the young person's own agreement (V3 legal commentary). Never record health, religion or union data in a strategy interview, because that is special-category data under Art. 6.
5. Voluntariness. Apprentices depend on their master and employer, and their contract runs through the parent. An apprentice's "yes" given in front of the master may not be free consent in the sense of KVKK Art. 3. Run sessions without the direct supervisor present, and let the young person decline without any effect on their training. This is my inference from KVKK Art. 3 and the voice research in section 3.
6. AI tools. If the stratej uses AI on interview notes, the ICMCI code (s. 3.4, 7.2, 8.3) says no personal data should go into tools that may train on it, and the client should agree explicitly first.
7. Who is responsible. The client firm is normally the data controller for its employees' data. The product vendor and an external stratej may be data processors or separate controllers depending on the contract. A lawyer must classify the roles.
