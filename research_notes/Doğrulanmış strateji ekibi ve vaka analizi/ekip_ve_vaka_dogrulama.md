## 1. Verification table for every in-scope flagged item

### Takeaway
41 items were checked. 27 moved up a level, 9 were corrected in substance, and 5 stay below V1 after all listed routes failed, each of those 5 has a verified substitute. The three biggest corrections are: cattle leather is no longer in the EU deforestation rule (since 18 September 2026), Labour Law 4857 Art. 4(f) excludes apprentices, so the 2004 child-worker regulation does not apply to them directly, and the "TAYSAD 32% plan capacity investment" figure is wrong (32% withdrew from bids).

## 3. Turkish law for apprentices and minors

### Takeaway
Apprentices are outside İş Kanunu (4857 Art. 4(f)) and are students under 3308 Art. 11. Their working conditions come from 3308, the MEB secondary-school regulation and Law 6331. KVKK decision 2020/622 lets a discerning child and the parent both use data rights, but says nothing on consent. A lawyer must confirm all of this.

## 5. Published Turkish teaching cases and academic case studies

### Takeaway
Two usable cases were found. The better match for Karar Atlası is a peer-reviewed case study of a 274-person, third-generation Istanbul hosiery exporter. The second is an Emerald teaching case on a Turkish family firm whose succession failed.

### Cited Findings
- Toker, K. and Görener, A. (2018). "Aile İşletmelerinde Yönetsel ve Operasyonel Sorunlar: Bir Örnek Olay İncelemesi". *İstanbul Ticaret Üniversitesi Girişimcilik Dergisi* 2(4): 87–105. Source: [DergiPark record](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/pub/tje/issue/54018/728387), [full text](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/1075478). Full text read (V1): the firm makes 40 million pairs of socks a year, has operated about 60 years, is on Istanbul's European side, sells in all Turkish regions and exports mainly to Europe and Canada (p. 91). Executives are third generation, the board is second generation, the second generation has the last word and resists changes the young executives propose with consultants (p. 91). Staff: 29 white-collar and 245 blue-collar on shifts (p. 95). Method: in-depth semi-structured interviews, weekly meetings with third-generation managers. Findings: managerial shortcomings in restructuring production flow and organisation, implicit conflict between generations.
- Dube, M. and Dube, S. (2021). "SomPack: succession planning gone wrong". *Emerald Emerging Markets Case Studies*, published 21 May 2021, 33 pages, DOI [10.1108/EEMCS-06-2019-0172](https://www.emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/EEMCS-06-2019-0172/full/html). Publisher overview (V2): the firm survived low-cost Asian competition, a revolt by extended family members who tried to bring it down with a competitor's help, the 2001 Turkish banking crisis and the 2008 crisis, the successor became CEO in 2004 and focused on cost cuts and discipline, which raised internal discontent and production-planning problems, by 2012 it considered new products with international partners, it collapsed suddenly in July 2013. Learning outcome: clear goals, sound practices and a systems view matter most in family firms, technology helps only when processes are defined. Firm size and sector not stated in the overview.
- HBS 119048 (Anadolu Group). See B12. It is a large group, not mid-size.

### Inferences
- The hosiery case fits Case 1 and Case 3: a second-generation board blocking third-generation executives is the "karar sahibi kim?" problem in a real, published setting of almost the same size (274 vs 150 to 220 staff).
- SomPack fits the "olası tuzaklar" parts: it shows that cost discipline without a shared plan can hide a collapse risk.

- IESE, INSEAD, Sabancı and Koç catalogues were not searched directly in this round (login walls in the earlier round).
