### Inferences
- **Karar's price sits inside Logo's management-module band, not the cheap SaaS band.** Karar (180,000 TL per year) ≈ 2.2× Logo Mind Insight Plan 3 first year (82,300) and 0.57× Mind Insight Plan 1 first year (316,200); ≈ 0.6× Logo's performance module for 100 employees on Plan 1 (294,600). It is 16× Paraşüt's regular annual list (11,280) and 4× Logo Cloud Standard (43,900). A mid-sized buyer that already pays Logo for ERP plus one analytics or HR module will see 180,000 TL as a familiar order of magnitude, while a buyer anchored on Paraşüt will see it as very expensive. CALC and INFERENCE.
- **Logo's "Esnek Ödeme Planı" is a ready pattern for Turkish buyers.** An upfront-heavy option (Plan 1, low renewal) versus a flat subscription (Plan 3) lets a family firm choose capex-like or opex-like payment. Karar could offer "pilot + year 1" upfront with a lower renewal, or a flat annual fee. INFERENCE.
- **Renewal at current list price is the Turkish norm.** Logo writes it into the price list. Karar should write "renewal at the then-current list price" into the order form, and publish its own list with an effective date (for example each January), with the right to issue a mid-year list, as Logo did in July 2026. INFERENCE.
- **Index guidance.** With CPI near 30% and PPI near 27%, a yearly TL increase tied to TÜFE or Yİ-ÜFE (or a USD/EUR list with TL invoicing) is needed to hold real price. A two-step increase (January and July) is locally normal (Logo, Turkcell). INFERENCE.
- Local OKR and performance vendors hide prices, so a public Karar price page would itself be a differentiator; Logo is the only local vendor with transparent management-software prices. INFERENCE.

## Q3. Retention and growth benchmarks by ACV band, CAC payback, services share

### Takeaway
SaaS Capital's 2025 survey (more than 1,000 private B2B SaaS firms) gives median NRR 98% and GRR 90% for ACV under USD 12k, which is Karar's band (≈ USD 3.6k–9.6k), versus 101% and 91% across all firms; multi-year contracts retain better (103% / 94%) than annual (101% / 90%) and monthly (100% / 89%). Benchmarkit's 2026 report shows a tougher 2025: median GRR 84%, seat-based NRR 98%, CAC payback 16 months. Karar should plan for about 90% gross retention as a "table stakes" floor and expect NRR below 100% unless a second decision or tier upgrade is sold.

### Cited Findings
- **SaaS Capital, Research Brief 32, "2025 B2B SaaS Retention Benchmarks"** (survey Q1 2025, 14th annual, more than 1,000 private B2B SaaS companies; ACV charts exclude companies under USD 1M ARR; PDF created 9 September 2025). — PRIMARY RESEARCH, [SaaS Capital 2025 retention PDF](https://www.saas-capital.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/RB32WS1-2025-B2B-SaaS-Retention-Benchmarks.pdf)

|---|---|---|---|---|
| < USD 12k | 98% | 90% | 90% | 106% |
| 12k–25k | 103% | 91% | 98% | 115% |

| 50k–100k | 104% | 90% | 96% | 110% |

## Q4. Willingness-to-pay methods with small samples (15–30 interviews)

### Takeaway
Van Westendorp (four price-perception questions) and Gabor-Granger (purchase intent at set prices) can be asked inside 15–30 buyer interviews, but at that size they give directional ranges, not a statistically valid optimum; conjoint needs about 300 respondents by Sawtooth's rule of thumb and is not feasible. The most credible small-sample signal is behavioural: a signed paid pilot at a stated price.

### Cited Findings
- Van Westendorp asks four questions and yields an acceptable price range; Gabor-Granger asks purchase likelihood at set prices and yields a demand curve and revenue-maximising price; vendor guides suggest 200–500 respondents per segment and recommend triangulating with real behaviour; B2B studies must reach the financial decision-maker. — THIRD PARTY, [Prospeo](https://prospeo.io/s/go-to-market-pricing-strategy), [CleverX](https://cleverx.com/blog/enterprise-b2b-willingness-to-pay-setting-and-defending-price) (earlier notes).
- Common practice: run Van Westendorp first to set the range, then Gabor-Granger on points inside it, with the same respondents. — THIRD PARTY, [Monetizely, VW vs GG](https://www.getmonetizely.com/articles/van-westendorp-vs-gabor-granger-for-saas-which-pricing-methodology-to-choose)
- Small-sample guidance found: "about 20 interviews are sufficient for most B2B enterprise SaaS studies"; WTP interviews of 10–15 sessions for a new product (5–8 for an annual review); B2B expert interviews with 12–20 decision-makers before threshold mapping; one blog claims a minimum of 15 respondents for Van Westendorp; other tables give 100–150 per segment. — THIRD PARTY vendor blogs, [Monetizely FAQ](https://www.getmonetizely.com/faqs/sample-sizes-for-reliable-pricing-research), [CleverX WTP data](https://cleverx.com/blog/collect-willingness-to-pay-data-b2b-buyers), [SIS International](https://www.sisinternational.com/zh_hk/solutions/qualitative-quantitative-research-solutions/van-westendorp-price-sensitivity-meter/), [dev.to](https://dev.to/alichherawalla/how-to-run-a-price-sensitivity-analysis-for-your-saas-1il5). No peer-reviewed minimum was found.
- Gabor-Granger in its simple form gives one data point per respondent, so it needs a bigger sample; progressive price ladders work better with small samples. — THIRD PARTY, [QuestionPro webinar Q&A](https://2019ac-the-opioid-epidemic.questionpro.com/webinar/pricing-research-webinar-introduction-and-tools.html)
- Conjoint (CBC): Sawtooth's rule of thumb is to start with 300 respondents (at least 200 per reported subgroup), each attribute level shown at least 500 times; these rules were built for pooled logit; HB and latent-class methods need fewer but no small-sample minimum is given. — PUBLISHED (method vendor), [Sawtooth, sample size rules of thumb](https://www.sawtoothsoftware.com/resources/blog/posts/sample-size-rules-of-thumb), [Sawtooth knowledge base](https://sawtoothsoftware.com/support/knowledge-base/design-and-methodology-issues/1343-determining-the-optimum-number-of-tasks-and-sample-size-in-cbc)

### Inferences (a protocol for 15–30 interviews; all INFERENCE, to be tested)
1. Interview only the payer (ikinci kuşak genel müdür or the board member who signs) and, separately, the buyer-operator (kurumsal gelişim or mali işler müdürü). Report the two groups separately; do not pool them.
2. Ask value questions first and record numbers: the decision's money at stake, the last strategy or consulting spend ("Son strateji çalışmanız size kaça mal oldu?"), and the cost of the last bad decision. This gives each respondent's own ROI anchor.
3. Ask Van Westendorp's four questions about the **yearly fee for one active decision**, not a monthly per-user price, so the frame matches the packaging.
4. Ask a Gabor-Granger ladder at three yearly prices that bracket the hypothesis, for example 108,000 / 180,000 / 300,000 TL (= 9,000 / 15,000 / 25,000 TL per month), with a 5-point intent scale. Start high and step down.
5. Close with a behavioural test: offer the 8-week pilot at a stated price and ask for a letter of intent or a signed order. Count signatures; treat them as the main WTP evidence.
6. Analyse as counts and ranges ("12 of 20 payers called 180,000 TL acceptable"), not as Van Westendorp intersection points or a fitted demand curve. With n < 30 per group, intersection points are unstable.
7. Use the Logo anchors (Mind Navigator, Mind Insight, j-HR performance module) as reference prices in the conversation only after the unanchored questions, to avoid biasing answers.

## Q5. Packaging examples for per-project, per-workspace or per-plan SaaS, and annual-indexed TL pricing
