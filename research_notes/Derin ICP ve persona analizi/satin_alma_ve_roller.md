## 1. Governance and decision making in Turkish family firms

### Takeaway
The best Turkey-specific governance figures are old (TKYD survey, 2017). About 60% of surveyed family firms had no family constitution and about 53% had no family council. About 56% had at least one non-family board member. Only 27% saw themselves as an institutionalised company. Succession is the biggest named difficulty, and formal succession plans are rare in every source found. No recent Turkish source with a stated sample size gives the share of professional (non-family) CEOs or of truly independent board members.

### Cited Findings
- TKYD "Aile Şirketleri Öncelikleri Anketi" (Turkish Corporate Governance Association), reported 14 October 2017. Sample size is not stated in the article. — [ORSİAD summary of the TKYD survey](https://orsiad.com.tr/her-iki-aile-sirketinden-biri-kurumsallasma-surecinde.html)
  - About 60% have no family constitution. 23% of main-shareholder families have a company constitution.
  - About 53% of main-shareholder families have no family council.
  - About 56% include non-family members on the board. Note: "non-family" is not the same as "independent".
  - About half are "still in the kurumsallaşma process". Only 27% see themselves as an institutional company.
  - 22% have written rules for family members' roles in management, such as succession criteria. 64% do not.
  - 48% name lack of succession planning as a threat. 37% list generational handover in future plans.
  - 52% rank professional management first among future plans. 46% name failure to professionalise as a threat.
  - 75% say finance is the most important agenda item. 33% have a shareholder policy. 72% have written job descriptions for employees. 37% list technological transformation in future plans.
  - Shareholding generation in the sample: 15% first generation, 21% second, 7% third (other shares not given).
  - The article says the biggest difficulty in institutionalisation is the handover to the next generation.
- KPMG global family business study, 2022: 2,439 family business representatives in 70 countries. Only 59% have a formal board and 24% have a family council. This is global, not Turkey-specific. — [KPMG Türkiye press release, June 2022](https://kpmg.com/tr/tr/home/medya/press-releases/2022/06/aile-sirketlerinin-yenileyici-gucu-arastirmasi.html) (search snippet)
- KPMG European Family Business Barometer 2018, Turkey cut: about 60% of Turkish family firms plan to hand management to the next generation. About 70% would consider a non-family CEO. Turkish sample size not found. — [KPMG Türkiye, Avrupa Aile Şirketleri Barometresi 2018](https://kpmg.com/tr/tr/home/gorusler/2018/11/avrupa-aile-sirketleri-barometresi-2018.html) (search snippet)
- PwC 12th Global Family Business Survey (published 2025): 1,325 owners and senior leaders in 60+ countries. 22% have a fully developed succession plan for all key roles, down from 26% two years earlier. 71% recognise the need to hire outside professionals for future leadership roles. 13% use AI or machine learning in a significant part of operations. Only 25% achieved double-digit growth, down from 43%. No Turkey sample size or Turkey cut was given in the press coverage. — [Ekonomim, 27 November 2025](https://www.ekonomim.com/is-dunyasi/aile-sirketlerinde-buyume-sert-geriledi-haberi-858244); [PwC Türkiye report page](https://www.pwc.com.tr/pwc-12-aile-sirketleri-arastirmasi)
- PwC Türkiye 2023 family business report (11th survey, Turkey edition): the report states that most Turkish family firm boards have no non-family members or members from other sectors. No percentage was retrievable. — [PwC Türkiye 11th survey page](https://www.pwc.com.tr/pwc-11-kuresel-aile-sirketleri-arastirmasi) (search snippet; PDF returned 403)
- A law-firm blog (5 November 2026) cites a "2023 study": boards made up entirely of family members are 59% in Turkey versus 36% globally. The original study is not named. Treat as unverified. — [bkaptan.com](https://www.bkaptan.com/2026/11/05/aile-sirketlerinde-kurumsal-yonetim-eksikligi-sorunlar-ve-hukuki-cozum-yollari/)
- Para Dergi (18 August 2026) on succession: cites a Deloitte global study where 35% name the next generation's lack of experience and leadership skill as the biggest obstacle, and 33% name not finding a suitable successor. It cites an İstanbul Ticaret University study saying about two-thirds of family firms do not complete the transfer to the second generation. It cites a study of İSO-registered family firm leaders where most have no formal succession plan (no figure, no sample size). — [Para Dergi](https://paradergi.com.tr/life-style/2026/08/18/aile-sirketlerinde-gelecek-dugumu-veliaht-var-lider-yok)

### Inferences
- The "separate decision owner" persona (founding generation or family council) is well supported in direction. Formal bodies such as family councils exist in a minority of firms. So the real decision owner is often a person (the founder or the senior shareholder), not a council. The product's "one decision owner per plan" rule fits this reality.
- Because only about a quarter of firms see themselves as institutionalised, "kurumsallaşma" is a live, named need. A written decision record and a handover report can be sold as a kurumsallaşma tool, not as a new strategy method.
- Finance tops the agenda (75% in TKYD). This supports the CFO or finance director as a likely champion where no strategy role exists.
- The KPMG 2018 figure (about 70% open to a non-family CEO) and the PwC 71% figure suggest professional general managers are a growing buyer type. That buyer may differ from the "second-generation GM" persona.

### Gaps
- No recent (2023–2026) Turkey-only survey with a stated sample size on family constitution adoption, family councils, independent board members or professional CEO share was found.
- TAİDER, TÜSİAD, Aile Şirketleri Platformu, Sabancı University and Koç University family business centre outputs were not reached in this session.
- No DergiPark or YÖK Tez study with a usable sample size on "who decides strategic matters" was retrieved. Full texts were not read.
- The TKYD 2021 "Türkiye'de Aile Şirketleri için Kurumsal Yönetim" report was downloaded but could not be read as text. It may hold newer figures. URL: https://www.tkyd.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/TKYD_Turkiyede-Aile-Sirketleri-Icin-Kurumsal-Yonetim_01112021.pdf

## 2. Management roles in 50–499 employee Turkish firms

### Takeaway
No source gives the prevalence of strategy, corporate development, CFO, quality or HR roles by firm size in Turkey. Salary portals show that "kurumsal gelişim", "organizasyonel gelişim" and "iş geliştirme" manager titles exist with enough postings to publish averages. Average monthly pay sits around 100,000–160,000 TL in 2025–2026. Posting counts were not found.

### Cited Findings
- Kurumsal Gelişim Yöneticisi, Kariyer.net: average monthly pay 158,518 TL in 2025 and 74,255 TL in 2024. The doubling suggests a small sample. — [Kariyer.net](https://www.kariyer.net/pozisyonlar/kurumsal+gelisim+yoneticisi/maas) (search snippet)
- Kurumsal Gelişim Yöneticisi, Yenibiris: average about 100,871 TL, range 80,697–249,833 TL. Year not stated in the snippet. — [Yenibiris](https://www.yenibiris.com/maaslar/kurumsal-gelisim-yoneticisi-maaslari) (search snippet)
- İş Geliştirme Müdürü, eleman.net, 2026: average 102,000 TL, minimum 70,600 TL, maximum 161,800 TL. — [eleman.net](https://www.eleman.net/meslek/is-gelistirme-muduru/maas) (search snippet)
- Organizasyonel Gelişim Müdürü, Kariyer.net, 2026: average monthly 111,460 TL. — [Kariyer.net](https://www.kariyer.net/pozisyonlar/organizasyonel+gelisim+muduru/maas) (search snippet)
- Kurumsal Finansman Müdürü salary page exists on Yenibiris; figure not captured. — [Yenibiris](https://www.yenibiris.com/maaslar/kurumsal-finansman-muduru-maaslari)
- TKYD 2017: 72% of surveyed family firms have written job descriptions for employees. This points to basic HR formalisation in larger family firms. — [ORSİAD summary](https://orsiad.com.tr/her-iki-aile-sirketinden-biri-kurumsallasma-surecinde.html)

### Inferences
- The champion persona title is real in the Turkish market. "Kurumsal gelişim" and "organizasyonel gelişim" are common Turkish titles, and they often blend strategy, process, quality and HR development. Persona 2 should list these titles together with "strateji ve iş geliştirme" and the finance director.
- At about 100,000–160,000 TL a month, one manager's monthly salary is a useful anchor for annual software pricing in TL.
- Where no such role exists, the TKYD finance priority suggests the finance director or the mali işler müdürü is the fallback champion.

### Gaps
- No posting counts for "kurumsal gelişim müdürü", "strateji müdürü" or "iş geliştirme müdürü" on Kariyer.net, LinkedIn Türkiye or Secretcv were retrieved. These sites need a direct, logged-out search, which was not done.
- No data on CFO or finance director prevalence by size class.
- No count of ISO 9001 certified Turkish firms and no data on quality manager prevalence were retrieved.
- No academic study on management role structure in 50–499 employee firms was found.

## 3. Software adoption: TÜİK data and ERP vendor landscape

### Takeaway
TÜİK's 2025 enterprise ICT survey shows a clear size step. Among firms with 50–249 employees, 46.0% use ERP, 18.4% CRM, 10.7% business intelligence and 31.8% paid cloud. Among firms with 250+ employees the figures are 76.5%, 42.0%, 35.1% and 54.3%. Enterprise AI use was 7.5% in 2025. The ERP market is led by SAP at the top and Logo (Tiger, GO, Netsis) in the mid-market. The last free IDC share data is from 2017. Mali müşavirs and e-Dönüşüm integrators strongly shape software choice in SMEs.

### Cited Findings
- TÜİK "Girişimlerde Bilişim Teknolojileri Kullanım Araştırması, 2025", released 11 September 2025, scope firms with 10+ employees. Sample size not given in the coverage. — [Alomaliye, 11 September 2025](https://www.alomaliye.com/2025/09/11/girisimlerde-bilisim-teknolojileri-kullanim-arastirmasi-2025/)

  | Indicator (2025 unless noted) | All 10+ | 10–49 | 50–249 | 250+ |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | ERP use | 28.3% | 23.6% | 46.0% | 76.5% |
  | CRM use | 12.0% | 9.9% | 18.4% | 42.0% |
  | Business intelligence use | 6.5% | 4.9% | 10.7% | 35.1% |
  | Paid cloud use | 20.4% | 17.3% | 31.8% | 54.3% |
  | Social media use | 55.2% | 52.8% | 64.2% | 83.4% |
  | Web or EDI e-sales (2024) | 13.6% | 12.9% | 15.1% | 26.5% |

  Paid cloud use was 16.4% in 2023. 56.5% of firms had a website in 2025.
- Enterprise AI use rose from 2.7% in 2021 to 7.5% in 2025, per TÜİK's first AI statistics release. — [Forbes Türkiye](https://www.forbes.com.tr/teknoloji/tuik-ilk-kez-acikladi-yapay-zekada-turkiye-nin-karnesi); [CNBC-e](https://www.cnbce.com/teknoloji/tuikten-ilk-kez-yapay-zeka-kullanimina-iliskin-istatistik-turkiyede-kac-kisi-uretken-yapay-zeka-kullaniyor-en-cok-hangi-sektor-kullaniyor-h17836) (search snippets). A 2026 news item reports a 37.6% figure, but it likely refers to individuals, not enterprises. Not verified. — [Yeni Ankara](https://www.yeniankara.com.tr/guncel/tuik-2026-yapay-zeka-verileri-kullanim-orani-yuzde-376ya-cikti-184785)
- Turkish ERP market size: 391.67 million USD in 2022 (IDC via Para Dergi). Last free share data, IDC 2017 enterprise applications: SAP 44.5%, Logo 22.7%, Microsoft 8.2%. — [erp-software.org Turkey page, research September 2026](https://erp-software.org/en/erp-worldwide/turkey/)
- IDC 2016 enterprise application software shares: SAP 42.2%, Logo 23.3%, Microsoft 8.4%. — [Ekonomist](https://www.ekonomist.com.tr/makale/yazilimda-umut-kurumsal-pazarda-412) (search snippet)
- Vendor tiers: SAP and Microsoft (via partners) at the top. Logo (Tiger, GO, Netsis), Mikro and Zirve (Mikrogrup, majority-owned by TeamSystem since 2023), Nebim (fashion retail), Uyumsoft, Workcube and canias ERP (IAS) in the local mid-market. Paraşüt, Logo İşbaşı and TÜRMOB's LUCA at entry level. — [erp-software.org](https://erp-software.org/en/erp-worldwide/turkey/)
- Logo list price for Tiger 3 Enterprise main package, one user: 817,900 TL excluding KDV, list of 7 January 2026. Logo resets its TL price list every 7 January. — [erp-software.org](https://erp-software.org/en/erp-worldwide/turkey/)
- VENDOR-SPONSORED: Logo's 40-year economic impact study says ERP use among KOBİs rose from 18.8% (2015) to 28.4% (2023). Logo's CEO said KOBİ ERP use reached 32.3% in 2025. — [Logo economic impact analysis, March 2025](https://cdn.logo.com.tr/files/logocomtr/Uploads/Documents/Logo-Yazilim-40-Yil-Ekonomik-Etki-Analizi-060325.pdf) (search snippet)
- A 2026 comparison blog claims Logo's group holds about 40% of the Turkish mid-market ERP segment. No analyst source. Treat as unverified. (Found in search; source not fetched.)
- Channel structure: local vendors such as Logo, Mikro, Uyumsoft and Zirve are also licensed e-Dönüşüm special integrators, so the ERP vendor often sells compliance in the same contract. Mali müşavirs often influence software choice in SMEs. Buyers may negotiate with three parties: vendor, integrator and mali müşavir. — [erp-software.org](https://erp-software.org/en/erp-worldwide/turkey/)
- e-Dönüşüm rules: e-Fatura is mandatory from 3 million TL gross turnover (500,000 TL in some sectors). e-Defter applies to all balance-sheet keepers from 1 January 2025. About 1.94 million e-Fatura users and 2.19 million e-Defter users at year-end 2025 (GİB data). — [erp-software.org](https://erp-software.org/en/erp-worldwide/turkey/)

### Inferences
- The 50–249 band is the tipping point. About half of these firms run an ERP, so financial and budget data often sits in Logo, Netsis or SAP. A Logo/Netsis read connector (actuals for budget versus plan) matters more than a Salesforce-style CRM connector.
- Only about one in ten firms with 50–249 employees uses BI software. Strategy reviews in this band likely run on Excel and slides. This supports the "Excel and presentation" pain in persona 2.
- About a third of 50–249 firms pay for cloud. Cloud SaaS is not yet the default here. On-premises or Turkey-hosted options and KVKK answers will come up in sales.
- Enterprise AI use is low (7.5%). AI should not be the lead promise for the economic buyer. This matches the existing report's advice.
- Logo partners, ERP resellers and mali müşavirs are a plausible channel, alongside consultants.

### Gaps
- No current independent (IDC, Gartner) KOBİ ERP shares for Logo, Mikro, Netsis, SAP Business One, Dynamics or canias.
- TÜİK 2025 sector-by-size breakdown for ERP, CRM, cloud and AI was not retrieved. TÜİK's own bulletin page was not fetched. Big data and analytics figures were not found.
- TÜİK sample size for the 2025 enterprise survey was not found in coverage.

## 4. Consulting market, fees and KOSGEB consulting support

### Takeaway
No current, reliable size for the Turkish management consulting market was found. The latest local estimate (about 10–12 billion TL, around 2017, attributed to YÖNDER) is old and pre-inflation. KOSGEB consulting support is small per firm: the YÖNDE programme caps digital transformation consulting at 20,000 TL per service and 40,000 TL in total. No published fee levels or project lengths for strategy or kurumsallaşma projects were found.

### Cited Findings
- The Turkish consulting sector was estimated at about 10–12 billion TL, growing about 10% a year, with the estimate attributed to the management consultants' association. The article appears to date from around 2017. It also says there is no clear count of consulting firms. — [Ekonomist](https://www.ekonomist.com.tr/makale/danismanlik-ihtiyaci-dijitallesmeyle-artiyor-2117) (search snippet)
- A Consultancy.uk article is titled "Turkish consulting market grows 8 percent to 410 million". Date and currency were not checked. — [Consultancy.uk](https://www.consultancy.uk/news/905/turkish-consulting-market-grows-8-percent-to-410-million) (title only, not fetched)
- An academic paper notes inconsistent figures on the sector's size. Another notes that the profession was formally defined by a 2013 national occupational standards communiqué. — [DergiPark](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/194599) (search snippet)
- KOSGEB YÖNDE (Yönderlik ve Değerlendirme) programme, directive dated July 2026: digital transformation consulting is supported up to 20,000 TL per service and 40,000 TL in total during the programme. — [KOSGEB YÖNDE directive, 2026](https://webdosya.kosgeb.gov.tr/Content/Upload/Dosya/YONDE/2026/2026.07.14/Y%C3%96NDE-Y%C3%B6nderlikveDe%C4%9FerlendirmeDestekProgram%C4%B1Y%C3%B6nergesi05.07.26.pdf) (search snippet)
- KOSGEB 2023 limits table: technical consulting support up to 40,000 TL, or 50,000 TL for firms in technoparks. May be outdated. — [KOSGEB limits table, 2023](https://webdosya.kosgeb.gov.tr/Content/Upload/Dosya/%C4%B0sletme%20Gelistirme%20Destek%20Program%C4%B1/2023/2023.06.02/TABLO-1_%C4%B0%C5%9Fletme_Geli%C5%9Ftirme_Destek_Program%C4%B1_Destek_%C3%9Cst_Limitleri_ve_Oranlar%C4%B1_Tablosu.pdf) (search snippet)
- KOSGEB KOBİGEL 2025 implementation rules exist. No consulting cap was extracted. — [KOBİGEL rules, 2025](https://webdosya.kosgeb.gov.tr/Content/Upload/Dosya/KOB%C4%B0GEL/2025/KOB%C4%B0GEL-KOB%C4%B0_Geli%C5%9Fim_Destek_Program%C4%B1_Uygulama_Esaslar%C4%B1.pdf)
- One secondary source says KOSGEB digital transformation grants cover licences and hardware but exclude consulting and training. This conflicts in part with the YÖNDE consulting support above; they may be different programmes. — [erp-software.org](https://erp-software.org/en/erp-worldwide/turkey/)

### Inferences
- KOSGEB consulting caps (20,000–40,000 TL) are far below one month of a strategy manager's salary. KOSGEB support will not fund a full strategy or kurumsallaşma project. It may fund a small diagnostic or a software licence, if the programme allows it.
- Consultants remain the likely way into mid-size family firms, but the market is fragmented and unmeasured. Channel sizing must come from primary interviews.

### Gaps
- No current YÖNDER member count, consultant count or market size.
- No published day rates or project fees for strategy or kurumsallaşma work.
- No typical project length data.
- KOSGEB rules on whether SaaS subscriptions count as eligible costs were not confirmed.

## 5. Procurement behaviour for B2B software in Turkish SMEs

### Takeaway
No independent survey on who approves software, trust in local versus foreign vendors, or demo and reference expectations was found. Structural facts are clear. Prices are set in TL and reset yearly. Post-dated cheques and payment terms are common in B2B trade. e-Dönüşüm compliance ties firms to their ERP vendor. KVKK cross-border transfer rules changed on 1 June 2024.

### Cited Findings
- Strategy& (PwC) published a July 2021 report on the KOBİ business operations software market, covering decision makers and top five purchase criteria. A search snippet suggests e-Dönüşüm influenced about 75% of cloud pre-accounting purchase decisions, but the text was garbled. Low confidence. Sponsor not confirmed; it may be vendor-commissioned. — [Strategy& 2021 PDF](https://www.strategyand.pwc.com/tr/tr/pdf/kobi-is-operasyonlari-yazilimi-pazar-ve-rekabet-ortami-analizi.pdf) (search snippet; PDF returned 403)
- Payment practice: post-dated cheques remain standard in B2B trade. From January to November 2025, 13.5 million cheques were presented and about 3.0% by value bounced (TBB data). Turkish Commercial Code article 1530 caps payment terms at 60 days where the creditor is an SME or the debtor a large firm. Contracts for movable goods must be paid in TL since 2022, with relaxations from March 2025. — [erp-software.org](https://erp-software.org/en/erp-worldwide/turkey/)
- Logo's maintenance subscription is tied to e-bookkeeping compliance. A 10% surcharge applies to renewals more than 30 days late. — [erp-software.org](https://erp-software.org/en/erp-worldwide/turkey/)
- VENDOR: Logo promoted flexible payment options for KOBİ digital transformation. — [Ekonomi Gazetesi](https://www.ekonomigazetesi.com/sirket-haberleri/logo-yazilimdan-kobilere-dijital-donusumde-esnek-odeme-destegi-78195) (title only)
- KVKK: since 1 June 2024 (law no. 7499), cross-border transfers follow a three-tier model: adequacy decision, appropriate safeguards such as standard contracts, or exemptions for occasional transfers. 1,345 standard contracts were notified by end-2024. Administrative fines reach up to 17,092,242 TL per breach in 2026. Banking (BDDK) requires domestic primary and secondary systems; no general data localisation rule for other sectors was found. — [erp-software.org](https://erp-software.org/en/erp-worldwide/turkey/)
- Vendor sites often claim KVKK requires personal data to stay in Turkey. This is not accurate after law 7499; transfer is allowed under conditions. — [Evosoft blog (vendor)](https://www.evosoft.com.tr/blog/bulut-erp-nedir-kobiler-icin-rehber)

### Inferences
- Price in TL, with an annual list and an option to pay in instalments or by term. USD pricing will meet resistance and may face contract-currency rules.
- Sales should expect the mali müşavir and the ERP partner to be consulted. They are influencers, not approvers.
- KVKK objections are likely. The product should state where data lives and which transfer basis applies. Many buyers hold the false belief that all data must stay in Turkey, so a Turkey-hosted option will remove friction even where it is not legally required.
- In family firms, the TKYD finance priority and founder-led decisions suggest that the owner or GM approves and finance negotiates terms. This is an inference, not survey data.

### Gaps
- No independent Turkish survey (BTK, TÜBİSAD, YASAD, Deloitte Türkiye) on SME software approvers, trust in local versus foreign vendors, demo and reference expectations, or local support SLAs was found.
- TÜBİSAD and YASAD sector reports were not reached.

## 6. Voice of the customer in Turkish business press

### Takeaway
Very little direct executive voice was collected. What exists repeats one theme: succession and next-generation readiness, not strategy tools. Dünya, Capital, HBR Türkiye and Fortune Türkiye panels were not reached in this session.

### Cited Findings
- PwC Türkiye's family business services leader said the 2025 survey shows family firms clearly diverging in performance and facing mounting pressure. — [Ekonomim, 27 November 2025](https://www.ekonomim.com/is-dunyasi/aile-sirketlerinde-buyume-sert-geriledi-haberi-858244)
- A Transearch Türkiye managing partner argued that the question is not who the heir is but how the heir becomes a leader. He advised heirs to gain outside experience, own real projects, and question founders' assumptions where needed. — [Para Dergi, 18 August 2026](https://paradergi.com.tr/life-style/2026/08/18/aile-sirketlerinde-gelecek-dugumu-veliaht-var-lider-yok)
- TKYD's 2017 survey summary says the biggest kurumsallaşma difficulty is the handover to the next generation. — [ORSİAD](https://orsiad.com.tr/her-iki-aile-sirketinden-biri-kurumsallasma-surecinde.html)
- A Dünya column treats the family constitution as a step in kurumsallaşma. — [Dünya](https://www.dunya.com/kose-yazisi/aile-anayasasi-ve-kurumsallasma-sureci/412582) (search result, not read)

### Inferences
- The persona trigger "takes over management" is well grounded. Succession is the most discussed pain. Messaging could frame the decision record as a way for the next generation to show its reasoning to the founding generation.
- The press frames the problem as leadership and governance, not as missing software. Karar Atlası should be positioned inside the kurumsallaşma and succession conversation.

### Gaps
- No Dünya, Capital, HBR Türkiye or Fortune Türkiye executive panel on strategy planning pain was read.
- No Turkish executive quote on strategy software buying was found.
