# Strategy team composition, participant expectations, strategist suitability and client readiness: what recognized authorities say (as of 16 November 2026)

Scope note. Research done on 16 November 2026 (Monday) with web search and fetch, about 45 tool calls. The product context is a Turkey-origin strategy-planning SaaS. Its first target is Turkish family-owned firms with 50 to 499 employees. In the product, the "stratej" (strategist) proposes options with reasoning. The decision owner chooses and records. The owner asked for what named authorities say, not for the assistant's own framework. Strategy update frequency is out of scope.

Source-status labels used below:
- **[PRIMARY-READ]** = I read the original text or the author's own page in this session.
- **[ABSTRACT]** = I saw only the publisher abstract or a bibliographic record.
- **[SECONDARY]** = I saw only a summary by someone else (book summary site, course notes, slides, blog). The writer should treat the wording as unverified.
- Items that rest only on background knowledge, with no page seen in this session, are kept in the Gaps sections, not in Cited Findings.

Two primary PDFs were downloaded to the session scratchpad and converted to text with macOS PDFKit: the HBR article by Kahneman, Lovallo and Sibony (2011), and the Turkish SBB public-sector guide (3rd version, 2018). Many HBR, Wiley, Sage and Elsevier pages were paywalled (HTTP 403 or teaser only). Those items are marked [ABSTRACT] or [SECONDARY].

Quotations are kept to a few short terms. Everything else is paraphrased.

## 1. Is strategic planning a team activity or one person's job?

### Takeaway
The authorities agree on a split. Many people should contribute knowledge and debate, but one clearly named person or body should make the final call and own it. Mintzberg moves planners into a support role for the managers who form strategy. Hamel, Lafley and Martin, Floyd and Wooldridge, and Hoshin Kanri all widen who contributes. Bain RAPID, the Netflix memo, Amazon's "disagree and commit" and Kahneman, Lovallo and Sibony all insist on one accountable decider. They also want the recommender kept separate from the decider. This split matches the product's model: the stratej recommends, and the owner decides.

### Cited Findings

**Mintzberg: planners support, managers form strategy**
- Mintzberg, *The Rise and Fall of Strategic Planning* (Free Press, 1994): planning rests on analysis and strategy rests on synthesis. That difference is his explanation for why formal strategic planning so often fails. [SECONDARY] — [Thinkers50, "Mintzberg on Strategy"](https://thinkers50.com/blog/mintzberg-on-strategy/); [WorldCat record](https://search.worldcat.org/oclc/28675146)
- In the same book, planners get supporting roles: finders of strategy, analysts, and catalysts for strategic thinking. A further section discusses "the planner as strategist". The new role of planning sits in support of strategy making, not inside it. [SECONDARY, from the table of contents and a review] — [Buresund book review](https://buresund.nu/books/the-rise-and-fall-of-strategic-planning); [eapad.dk](https://eapad.dk/book/the-rise-and-fall-of-strategic-planning/)
- Mintzberg also argues that effective strategists stay close to daily detail. They do not abstract themselves from it. [SECONDARY] — [Thinkers50](https://thinkers50.com/blog/mintzberg-on-strategy/)

**Hamel: strategy making should be democratic**
- Hamel, "Strategy as Revolution", *Harvard Business Review* 74(4), July–August 1996, pp. 69–82 or 69–83 (sources differ). The opening argues that firms have reached the limits of incremental improvement. [PRIMARY-READ, opening paragraph only] — [HBR](https://hbr.org/1996/07/strategy-as-revolution); [London Business School record](https://london.edu/faculty-and-research/academic-research/s/strategy-as-revolution-p682)
- One of Hamel's principles is that strategy making must be democratic. He names three under-represented groups as especially important: young people, newcomers and people at the geographic periphery. [SECONDARY, chapter summary] — [O'Reilly, "The Truth About..." chapter 36](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/the-truth-about/9780273723561/html/chapter-036.html)
- A 1996 profile of Hamel reports his remedy as involving more young people and more people at the geographic periphery. His reason is that people far from headquarters have fewer resources and more room to experiment. [SECONDARY] — [Business Standard, 1997 archive](https://www.business-standard.com/article/specials/the-dirty-little-secret-of-strategy-197051301096_1.html)
- Conflict to flag: the HBR article is usually described as ten principles, but the 1996 Business Standard profile describes "four rules". I could not resolve this without the full text. — [Fundación Mapfre index](https://documentacion.fundacionmapfre.org/documentacion/publico/i18n%20/bib/50233.do); [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/article/specials/the-dirty-little-secret-of-strategy-197051301096_1.html)
- C. K. Prahalad, in an interview on the same theme, argues that people lower in the hierarchy should help formulate strategy. These people are closer to new technology, customers and competitors. [SECONDARY] — [strategy+business](https://www.strategy-business.com/article/17774)

**Lafley and Martin: choices at every level**
- Lafley and Martin, *Playing to Win: How Strategy Really Works* (Harvard Business Review Press, 2013). Strategy is a set of five linked choices: winning aspiration, where to play, how to win, core capabilities, and management systems. Large organizations have nested cascades of these choices at corporate, category and brand level. Lower-level choices feed back into the choices above. Strategy is therefore not reserved for the CEO. [SECONDARY] — [Farnam Street](https://fs.blog/playing-to-win-how-strategy-really-works/); [Shortform](https://www.shortform.com/blog/playing-to-win-how-strategy-really-works/)

**Floyd and Wooldridge: middle managers' strategic roles**
- Floyd and Wooldridge, *The Strategic Middle Manager* (Jossey-Bass). The research base is more than 250 middle managers. The book names four strategic roles: championing alternatives, synthesizing information, facilitating adaptability, and implementing deliberate strategy. The first two are upward influence, and the last two are downward influence. [SECONDARY, publisher synopsis and table of contents] — [Blackwell's synopsis](https://blackwells.co.uk/bookshop/product/The-Strategic-Middle-Manager-by-Steven-W-Floyd-Bill-Wooldridge/9780787902087); [Kisch table of contents](https://www.kisch-online.de/details/9780787902087/the-strategic-middle-manager-steven-w-floyd/)
- A later paper summarizes Floyd and Wooldridge's 1992 and 1996 work as setting out these four roles. It notes that upward influence (synthesizing and championing) was reported less often than the other behaviours. [SECONDARY] — [Brunel University paper](https://bura.brunel.ac.uk/bitstream/2438/18065/2/FullText.pdf)

**Kaplan and Norton: Office of Strategy Management**
- Kaplan and Norton, "The Office of Strategy Management", *Harvard Business Review* 83(10), October 2005, pp. 72–80. The abstract says most companies have a gap between strategy formulation and strategy execution. It cites implementation failure rates of 60 to 90 percent from other sources. [ABSTRACT] — [PubMed 16250626](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16250626/)
- An HBS summary quotes the article: 95 percent of employees in most organizations do not understand their strategy. It also cites a Bain study of 1,854 large firms in which seven of eight failed to achieve profitable growth. [SECONDARY] — [HBS Working Knowledge](https://hbswk.hbs.edu/item/the-office-of-strategy-management)
- Conflict to flag: other web sources attribute different "employees understand strategy" figures (5 percent, 7 percent) to Kaplan and Norton. These do not match the 95 percent figure in the HBS summary. — [ClearPoint statistics page](https://www.clearpointstrategy.com/strategic-planning-statistics)

**Hoshin Kanri and catchball**
- The Lean Enterprise Institute lexicon defines strategy deployment (hoshin kanri, policy deployment) as aligning functions and activities with strategic objectives, both vertically and horizontally. As a lean programme matures, proposals increasingly come from each part of the organization, bottom up to top. The entry is adapted from Pascal Dennis (2006). [PRIMARY-READ via search extract] — [LEI lexicon, "Strategy Deployment"](https://www.lean.org/lexicon-terms/strategy-deployment/)
- Catchball is the back-and-forth step in which each level receives proposed objectives, challenges them and returns revised plans before anyone commits. [SECONDARY, practitioner blogs] — [KaiNexus blog](https://blog.kainexus.com/improvement-disciplines/lean/catchball/the-role-of-catchball-in-strategic-planning); [Lean Blog, March 2026](https://www.leanblog.org/2026/03/strategy-deployment-catch-ball-chuck-rock/)
- Akao's book is the usual reference (*Hoshin Kanri: Policy Deployment for Successful TQM*). Sources give 1988 or 1989 for the Japanese original. [SECONDARY] — [Rework summary](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/strategic-management/hoshin-kanri)

**One accountable decider: Bain RAPID**
- Bain RAPID roles, from Bain's own tool page (13 October 2023). Recommend: gathers input and builds a proposal for the decider; one recommender per decision. Agree: confirms the proposal is feasible (for example legal or regulatory); the decider settles disputes between Agree and Recommend. Perform: carries out the decision. Input: supplies expertise or information, including people affected by the decision, who should feel heard even if their input is not used. Decide: makes the final call, commits the organization and is accountable. [PRIMARY-READ] — [Bain, RAPID tool](https://www.bain.com/insights/rapid-tool-to-clarify-decision-accountability/)
- Bain says each decision should ideally have one decider. If a group must decide, it should agree in advance how to decide without consensus, for example by majority or by the chair. Agree roles should be given sparingly. [PRIMARY-READ] — [Bain, RAPID tool](https://www.bain.com/insights/rapid-tool-to-clarify-decision-accountability/)
- The original article is Rogers and Blenko, "Who Has the D? How Clear Decision Roles Enhance Organizational Performance", *HBR*, January 2006. Only the introduction was readable. [ABSTRACT] — [HBR](https://hbr.org/2006/01/who-has-the-d-how-clear-decision-roles-enhance-organizational-performance)

**Netflix: informed captain and farming for dissent**
- The Netflix culture memo (undated page; it refers to Q1 2024 figures and says the memo was first written in 2009). Each significant decision has one "informed captain" who makes the final call. Netflix avoids decision by committee. Captains are expected to seek out different opinions at every level, and opinions are weighted by expertise. After the decision, everyone is expected to "disagree then commit". The captain then reflects on what worked. [PRIMARY-READ] — [Netflix culture memo](https://jobs.netflix.com/culture)
- The book behind it is Hastings and Meyer, *No Rules Rules: Netflix and the Culture of Reinvention* (2020). An HBS article describes the informed-captain model as a way to speed decisions. [SECONDARY] — [HBS Working Knowledge](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/freedom-fear-and-feedback-should-other-companies-follow-netflixs-lead)

**Amazon: disagree and commit, two-way doors**
- Bezos, 2016 letter to shareholders (published 2017). Many decisions are reversible "two-way doors" and can use a light process. Most decisions should be made with about 70 percent of the information one wishes for; waiting for 90 percent is usually too slow. "Disagree and commit" saves time: a leader without consensus can ask the team to commit anyway, and Bezos says he does the same with his teams' decisions. [PRIMARY-READ] — [Amazon, 2016 letter to shareholders](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/company-news/2016-letter-to-shareholders)

**Separation of recommender and decider (Kahneman, Lovallo, Sibony)**
- Kahneman, Lovallo and Sibony, "Before You Make That Big Decision...", *HBR* 89(6), June 2011, pp. 50–60. Decision quality control assumes a real separation between the decision maker and the team making the recommendation. If the executive shapes the proposal in advance, for example by choosing team members with known views or signalling a preference, he becomes a de facto member of the recommending team. He can then no longer judge the proposal. A warning sign is that implementation has begun before the decision. [PRIMARY-READ, full text] — [PubMed 21714386](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21714386/); [HBR](https://hbr.org/2011/06/the-big-idea-before-you-make-that-big-decision)

**Public-sector comparison (Turkey)**
- The SBB guide says strategic planning must not be seen as the job of one unit or one person. Preparing the plan and managing by it is the basic responsibility of top management, and top-manager support is an indispensable condition. [PRIMARY-READ] — [Kamu İdareleri İçin Stratejik Planlama Kılavuzu, 3rd version, 2018, p. 8](https://sgb.adalet.gov.tr/Resimler/SayfaDokuman/322020141843kamuidareleri.pdf)
- The same guide notes that the strategy development unit (SGB) coordinates the plan but does not write it. [PRIMARY-READ] — [SBB guide, p. 9](https://sgb.adalet.gov.tr/Resimler/SayfaDokuman/322020141843kamuidareleri.pdf)

### Inferences
- Across the sources, "team activity" means wide input and debate. It does not mean shared decision rights. RAPID, Netflix and Amazon all keep one decider. In the product, the owner maps to "Decide", the stratej to "Recommend", and staff to "Input". This mapping is my inference, not a claim by Bain.
- Kahneman et al. give a direct reason to keep the stratej's recommendation work separate from the owner's decision. If the owner steers the recommendation early, the review loses value.
- Mintzberg's planner roles (finder, analyst, catalyst) are close to the stratej's job in the product. The product should not present the stratej as the one who forms the strategy.

### Gaps
- Mintzberg's 1994 HBR article "The Fall and Rise of Strategic Planning" (January–February 1994) was not reached. Mintzberg and Waters (1985), "Of Strategies, Deliberate and Emergent", *Strategic Management Journal* 6(3), is relevant to "strategy formed by many", but I did not open it this session.
- Floyd and Wooldridge (1992), *SMJ* 13 (special issue), and Wooldridge and Floyd (1990, *SMJ*) and Floyd and Wooldridge (1997, *Journal of Management Studies* 34(3)) were not opened. Their performance findings (upward influence and firm performance) rest on my background knowledge only. Check before use. The book *Building Strategy from the Middle* (Sage, 2000) was also not verified. The publication year of *The Strategic Middle Manager* was not shown on the pages seen (background knowledge says 1996).
- Drucker on who should plan: no primary text found this session. See section 5 for his dissent rule.
- Jackson, *Hoshin Kanri for the Lean Enterprise* (2006), was not reached.
- No study found that measures "team versus single-person" strategic planning outcomes in family SMEs.

## 2. Who should be in the team? Composition, size, family-business bodies

### Takeaway
No authority found gives a fixed headcount for a private firm. Sources give principles instead. Bring the decision makers and the people who hold key knowledge. Represent the main units. Keep the group small enough to decide and large enough to show different views. Keep membership stable. For family firms, Carlock and Ward plan the family track and the business track in parallel, starting from family values. Gersick et al. separate the family, ownership and management roles. The Turkish SBB guide has a two-layer structure: a board that approves, and a team that does the work. It is a useful comparison but it is written for public bodies.

### Cited Findings

**Family business**
- Carlock and Ward, *Strategic Planning for the Family Business: Parallel Planning to Unify the Family and Business* (Palgrave, 2001). The authors argue that estate planning and succession are too narrow as planning goals. Their Parallel Planning Process runs a family track and a business track. It starts from family values and business philosophy, and it ends in a shared vision of the family and the business. Family and business plans get equal weight. [SECONDARY, publisher text, table of contents, summaries] — [getAbstract summary](https://www.getabstract.com/en/summary/strategic-planning-for-the-family-business/2071); [Kellogg record](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/faculty/research/detail/2001/strategic-planning-for-the-family-business-parallel-planning-to?p=1); [Family Business Consulting Group resource page](https://www.thefbcg.com/resource/strategic-planning-for-the-family-business-parallel-planning-to-unify-the-family-and-business/)
- Gersick, Davis, McCollom Hampton and Lansberg, *Generation to Generation: Life Cycles of the Family Business* (Harvard Business School Press, 1997). The book builds on the three-circle model (family, ownership, business). That model separates ownership from management. The book adds a developmental view: ownership moves from controlling owner to sibling partnership to cousin consortium. [SECONDARY] — [Family Business Magazine](https://familybusinessmagazine.com/engagement/education-development/how-three-circles-changed-way-we-understand-family-business/); [Perlego record](https://www.perlego.com/book/837037/generation-to-generation-life-cycles-of-the-family-business-pdf)
- Conflict to flag: one source dates the three-circle model to 1978 (Tagiuri and Davis), and another to Davis's 1982 dissertation. — [Family Business Magazine](https://familybusinessmagazine.com/engagement/education-development/how-three-circles-changed-way-we-understand-family-business/); [Rework summary](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/family-business/three-circle-model-family-business)

**Team qualities and size (SBB guide, public sector, comparison only)**
- Strategy Development Board (Strateji Geliştirme Kurulu): chaired by the top manager, with deputy top managers and spending-unit heads, plus others the top manager appoints. It approves the planning team and the preparation programme, checks main stages and outputs, secures unit participation and decides disputed points. It also finalizes mission, vision, values, goals and target cards from alternative drafts. [PRIMARY-READ] — [SBB guide, p. 9](https://sgb.adalet.gov.tr/Resimler/SayfaDokuman/322020141843kamuidareleri.pdf)
- Strategic Planning Team (Stratejik Planlama Ekibi): led by a deputy of the top manager, coordinated by the SGB, made of spending-unit representatives and the SGB head. Field units may join. The top manager is its natural head and may chair it. [PRIMARY-READ] — [SBB guide, p. 9](https://sgb.adalet.gov.tr/Resimler/SayfaDokuman/322020141843kamuidareleri.pdf)
- Each team member should be able to represent their unit, bring relevant knowledge and experience, work well with others, and give enough time. The team as a whole should represent the main service units and the SGB, mix different specialties and keep members for the whole effort. [PRIMARY-READ] — [SBB guide, p. 10](https://sgb.adalet.gov.tr/Resimler/SayfaDokuman/322020141843kamuidareleri.pdf)
- On size, the guide gives a rule, not a number. The team should not be so large that joint decisions become impossible. It should not be so small that different ideas and units go unrepresented. [PRIMARY-READ] — [SBB guide, p. 10](https://sgb.adalet.gov.tr/Resimler/SayfaDokuman/322020141843kamuidareleri.pdf)
- The team may form sub-working groups with experienced staff from units. These groups do parts of the situation analysis under team-member leadership. [PRIMARY-READ] — [SBB guide, pp. 10–12](https://sgb.adalet.gov.tr/Resimler/SayfaDokuman/322020141843kamuidareleri.pdf)

**Bryson (public and nonprofit)**
- Bryson's initial agreement typically outlines a strategic planning task force or coordinating committee. It also guarantees resources and signals political support from key decision makers. A sequence of initial agreements with a widening group of decision makers may be needed. [SECONDARY, course slides] — [Slideshare, Bryson chapter 3 slides](https://www.slideshare.net/robintgreene/bryson-chapter-3-initiating-and-agreeing-on-a-strategic-planning-process)

**Decision roles as a composition tool**
- Bain RAPID lets a firm assign Recommend, Agree, Perform, Input and Decide per decision. Agree roles should be few. [PRIMARY-READ] — [Bain, RAPID tool](https://www.bain.com/insights/rapid-tool-to-clarify-decision-accountability/)

**Diversity of the recommending team**
- Kahneman et al. (2011) warn that groupthink is especially likely when the team has little diversity of background and viewpoint. [PRIMARY-READ] — [HBR 2011 via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21714386/)
- Hodgkinson et al. (2006) found that strategy workshops typically exclude middle managers. They see this as reinforcing an elitist approach to strategy. [ABSTRACT] — [Southampton ePrints record](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/37496)

### Inferences
- For a 150-person shoe manufacturer, the SBB two-layer model translates roughly to: owner (and any family owners) as the deciding body, and a working team of unit leads (export lead, design lead, accountant, a workshop master, a subcontractor follower). This is my translation of the SBB structure, not something the guide says about private firms.
- Carlock and Ward suggest that family matters (values, ownership, who works in the firm) need their own track. In a Turkish family firm this may be separate from the management workshop.
- The size rule ("large enough to represent, small enough to decide") is the only size guidance found from an authority.

### Gaps
- Carlock and Ward's exact description of family council, family meeting, board and management roles in the Parallel Planning Process was not readable (HBS Working Knowledge page returned 403). Their later book, *When Family Businesses Are Best* (2010), was not checked.
- No authority found gives a recommended headcount for a core strategy team in a 50 to 499 employee firm. Practitioner figures such as "8 to 12 people" circulate online, but I found no named, primary source, so none is reported.
- Bryson's own team guidance (for example a "strategic planning coordinating committee" and a "strategic planning team") was seen only in course slides.

## 3. Front-line and shop-floor input

### Takeaway
Several authorities argue for front-line input. Hamel and Prahalad want people closer to customers and technology involved. Toyota's genchi genbutsu sends managers to the real place. The SBB guide requires participation from every level. The empirical base is thinner. Ketokivi and Castañer (2004) show that participation plus communication reduces middle managers' narrow "position bias" in manufacturing plants. Strategy-as-practice research shows that workshops usually exclude lower levels. It also explains how language about strategy keeps people out. Open-strategy research names real dilemmas of opening up. Edmondson shows that psychological safety drives learning behaviour in work teams. No source found studies apprentices or shop-floor workers in strategy making directly.

### Cited Findings

**Argument for the periphery**
- Hamel (1996): involve young people, newcomers and the geographic periphery. [SECONDARY] — [O'Reilly chapter 36](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/the-truth-about/9780273723561/html/chapter-036.html)
- Prahalad: people lower in the hierarchy, closer to customers, competitors and technology, should help formulate strategy. [SECONDARY] — [strategy+business](https://www.strategy-business.com/article/17774)
- Netflix memo: captains should seek opinions at every level, weighted by expertise. [PRIMARY-READ] — [Netflix culture memo](https://jobs.netflix.com/culture)

**Toyota: go and see**
- Genchi genbutsu is Principle 12 in Liker's *The Toyota Way* (McGraw-Hill, 2004): go and see for yourself to understand the situation thoroughly. [SECONDARY, chapter title on O'Reilly] — [O'Reilly, *The Toyota Way*, chapter 18](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/the-toyota-way/9780071392310/chapter18.html)
- Toyota set out its internal "Toyota Way 2001". A blog quoting Toyota's current official page says the company goes to the source to find facts, make correct decisions and build consensus. A later "Toyota Way 2020" exists. [SECONDARY; Toyota's own page returned 404] — [Bob Emiliani blog](https://bobemiliani.com/?p=29995); [TechTarget definition](https://www.techtarget.com/whatis/definition/Genchi-Genbutsu)

**Evidence on participation**
- Ketokivi and Castañer, "Strategic Planning as an Integrative Device", *Administrative Science Quarterly* 49(3), 2004, pp. 337–365. Data: 164 manufacturing plants in five countries and three industries. Employee participation in planning and communication of the agreed goals work together to reduce managers' position bias, meaning their focus on goals tied to their own function. [ABSTRACT] — [EconBiz record](https://econbiz.de/Record/strategic-planning-as-an-integrative-device-ketokivi-mikko/10014190596); [Castañer page](https://people.unil.ch/xaviercastaner/?p=40)
- Conflict to flag: the 2003 working paper version reported that communication, but not involvement, reduced position bias. The published 2004 version reports the complementary effect. Cite the published version. — [IDEAS/RePEc working paper](https://ideas.repec.org/p/ebg/heccah/0775.html)
- Mantere and Vaara, "On the Problem of Participation in Strategy: A Critical Discursive Perspective", *Organization Science* 19(2), 2008, pp. 341–358. Data from strategy work in 12 organizations. Three discourses tend to block participation: mystification, disciplining and technologization. Three discourses support it: self-actualization, dialogization and concretization. [ABSTRACT] — [EconPapers record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:inm:ororsc:v:19:y:2008:i:2:p:341-358); [Hanken portal](https://research.hanken.fi/en/publications/on-the-problem-of-participation-in-strategy-a-critical-discursive/)
- Hautz, Seidl and Whittington, "Open Strategy: Dimensions, Dilemmas, Dynamics", *Long Range Planning* 50(3), 2017, pp. 298–309. Open strategy varies along two dimensions, transparency and inclusion. The paper names five dilemmas: process, commitment, disclosure, empowerment and escalation. Firms may move back towards less openness. [ABSTRACT] — [CVTISR record](https://ersearch2.cvtisr.sk/vufind/SummonRecord/FETCH-LOGICAL-c329t-3321e037a3498294acdba9dbb0b5ca6b3763c1eaae36892d47b9e3a87a472d453/Details)
- An AXA UK and Ireland case document describes open strategy as a continuing balance of how open to be. [SECONDARY, practitioner case] — [University of Zurich, AXA UKI case PDF](https://www.business.uzh.ch/dam/jcr:6f131156-542f-4de9-9b0b-df28d73ff8b5/Open%20Strategy%20at%20AXA%20UKI_A%20new%20approach%20to%20strategy%20making.pdf)

**Psychological safety**
- Edmondson, "Psychological Safety and Learning Behavior in Work Teams", *Administrative Science Quarterly* 44(2), June 1999, pp. 350–383. Team psychological safety is a shared belief that the team is safe for interpersonal risk taking. In 51 work teams in a manufacturing company, psychological safety was linked to learning behaviour. Learning behaviour mediated the link to team performance. Context support and leader coaching mattered. [ABSTRACT] — [SAGE DOI page](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.2307/2666999); [Harvard DASH](https://dash.harvard.edu/entities/publication/13a7b031-0fdd-45ec-a7e0-2b80e2bc679f)

**How to collect input safely**
- Kahneman et al. (2011): if dissent was stifled, the decision maker may discreetly ask members of the recommending team for dissenting views, for example in private meetings. People who resisted conformity pressure deserve special attention. Long term, leaders should make substantive disagreement a normal, objectively resolved part of decisions. [PRIMARY-READ] — [HBR 2011 via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21714386/)
- SBB guide: internal stakeholders include employees and managers. Methods include surveys and workshops (çalıştay). A survey can be used first to choose topics for a workshop. The culture analysis asks how far employees take part in decisions and whether mechanisms for participation exist. Methods named include search conferences, surveys and interviews. [PRIMARY-READ] — [SBB guide, pp. 15–23](https://sgb.adalet.gov.tr/Resimler/SayfaDokuman/322020141843kamuidareleri.pdf)

### Inferences
- For the shoe manufacturer, the sources support asking masters, journeymen, apprentices, warehouse staff and subcontractor followers for input, because they are close to the work. They do not support giving them decision rights. In RAPID terms, they are "Input". This is my mapping.
- Edmondson and Kahneman et al. together suggest private or anonymous channels when hierarchy is steep, as in master–apprentice relations. Edmondson's study was of teams, not of strategy input, so this is an extension.
- Ketokivi and Castañer's result is about plant middle managers, not shop-floor workers. Using it for apprentices goes beyond the evidence.

### Gaps
- No study found on apprentices or shop-floor workers taking part in strategy formulation, in Turkey or elsewhere.
- No primary source found that tests anonymity in strategy input. Edmondson's later book *The Fearless Organization* (2018) was not checked.
- Stieger et al. (2012, *California Management Review*) on crowdsourcing strategy at Hirschmann, and Whittington, Cailluet and Yakis-Douglas (2011, *British Journal of Management*) on opening strategy, were not opened this session.
- Toyota's own Toyota Way page could not be reached (404), so the official wording was not verified.

## 4. Strategy workshops research

### Takeaway
Strategy workshops are a common part of formal planning. A large UK survey (1,337 returns) found that workshops rely on discussion more than analysis. They usually exclude middle managers. Johnson et al. (2010) explain why some workshops meet their purpose and others do not. Three ritual features matter: how far the event is removed from daily work, its liturgy (set routines and scripts), and the role of specialists such as facilitators.

### Cited Findings
- Hodgkinson, Whittington, Johnson and Schwarz, "The Role of Strategy Workshops in Strategy Development Processes: Formality, Communication, Co-ordination and Inclusion", *Long Range Planning* 39(5), 2006, pp. 479–496. Survey of UK managers, 1,337 returns. Workshops play an important part in formal strategic planning. They favour discussion over analytical tools. They typically exclude middle managers, which reinforces an elitist approach. The authors still see workshops as important vehicles for strategy to emerge. [ABSTRACT] — [Southampton ePrints record](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/37496); [UTM library record](https://libmast.utm.my/Record/oxford-uuid:0e8d5f91-495e-4d4b-a305-f995262071dd)
- The same group published an earlier AIM/CMI report: "The Role and Importance of Strategy Workshops: Findings of a UK Survey" (2005). [ABSTRACT] — [EconBiz record](https://www.econbiz.de/Record/the-role-and-importance-of-strategy-workshops-findings-of-a-uk-survey-hodgkinson/10009458557)
- One secondary source says the survey went to 8,000 AIM members. That source is a student course document, so the figure is unverified. — [Scribd course document](https://www.scribd.com/document/456098076/6-7-strategy-workshops-doc)
- Johnson, Prashantham, Floyd and Bourque, "The Ritualization of Strategy Workshops", *Organization Studies* 31(12), 2010, pp. 1589–1618 (page ranges differ by source), DOI 10.1177/0170840610376146. Earlier research lacked a theory of why some workshops achieve their purpose. Using ritual theory, the authors find that removal from everyday work, use of liturgy, and the role of specialists shape behaviour in the workshop and so its outcome. [ABSTRACT] — [Lancaster ePrints](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/45789); [Glasgow ePrints](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/40116)
- Note for the writer: the brief calls this "Johnson et al. (2010)" without a journal. It is in *Organization Studies*, not *Long Range Planning*.

### Inferences
- The workshop findings suggest the product should record who attended, what evidence was brought, and what follow-up was agreed. The research says discussion dominates and analysis is thin. This is my inference for product design.
- Johnson et al.'s three features fit the product's "stratej" role: a specialist who sets a clear routine and separates the session from daily work.

### Gaps
- The full texts of Hodgkinson et al. (2006) and Johnson et al. (2010) were not available. Their detailed findings on preparation, duration, facilitator use, follow-up and failure causes are therefore not reported here.
- Jarzabkowski's work (for example Jarzabkowski and Seidl 2008 on strategy meetings, *Organization Studies*) was not opened this session.
- No workshop research on family firms or on Turkey was found.

## 5. Participant expectations: before, during and after

### Takeaway
The authorities expect participants to bring evidence, to voice dissent before the decision, and to commit after it. Kahneman, Lovallo and Sibony give a 12-question checklist for whoever reviews a recommendation. Lovallo and Sibony found that a good debate process mattered about six times more than analysis. Janis's groupthink remedies include an impartial leader, a devil's advocate, outside experts and a second-chance meeting. Drucker says not to decide without disagreement. Netflix and Amazon expect people to disagree, then commit. The SBB guide asks team members for enough time and continuity.

### Cited Findings

**Decision-quality checklist (Kahneman, Lovallo, Sibony 2011)** [PRIMARY-READ, paraphrased] — [HBR 2011 via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21714386/); [HBR](https://hbr.org/2011/06/the-big-idea-before-you-make-that-big-decision)
- Questions decision makers ask themselves:
  1. Could the recommending team's self-interest bias the proposal? (Do not ask them this directly.)
  2. Have the recommenders fallen in love with their proposal?
  3. Was there dissent inside the recommending team, and was it explored?
- Questions to put to the recommenders:
  4. Is the diagnosis driven by a salient analogy?
  5. Were credible alternatives considered?
  6. If you had to decide again in a year, what information would you want, and can you get it now?
  7. Where did the numbers come from?
  8. Is there a halo effect?
  9. Are the recommenders too attached to past decisions?
- Questions about the proposal:
  10. Is the base case overly optimistic?
  11. Is the worst case bad enough?
  12. Is the recommending team overly cautious?
- When to use it: for decisions that are important and recurring enough to justify a formal process, such as a large capital outlay or a mid-size acquisition. It is not for routine decisions that are rubber-stamped.
- How to use it: completely and systematically. The authors cite Gawande: partial use of a checklist can fail.
- Warning: a set of options with only one realistic alternative, the one the team prefers, needs extra scrutiny.

**Process quality matters more than analysis (Lovallo and Sibony 2010)**
- Lovallo and Sibony, "The Case for Behavioral Strategy", *McKinsey Quarterly*, March 2010. They studied 1,048 major decisions over five years, including new products, M&A and large capital spending. Process mattered more than analysis by a factor of about six. Analysis still mattered: almost no strong-process decisions had very poor analysis. On a subset of 673 decisions with ROI data, moving from bottom to top quartile on process raised ROI by 6.9 percentage points; for analysis the figure was 5.3 points. [SECONDARY; the McKinsey page timed out] — [McKinsey Quarterly](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/strategy-and-corporate-finance/our-insights/the-case-for-behavioral-strategy); [Farnam Street summary](https://fs.blog/what-matters-more-in-decisions-analysis-or-process/)

**Groupthink (Janis)**
- Janis, *Victims of Groupthink* (1972); revised as *Groupthink* (1982). Remedies attributed to Janis: the leader stays impartial and does not state a preference first; one or more devil's advocates at every meeting; outside experts invited to challenge the group; a second-chance meeting after preliminary consensus to air remaining doubts. [SECONDARY] — [SAGE Encyclopedia of Management Theory, "Groupthink"](https://sk.sagepub.com/ency/edvol/embed/encyclopedia-of-management-theory/chpt/groupthink); [Regent University journal](https://www.regent.edu/journal/emerging-leadership-journeys/groupthink-theory/)
- Caveat: reviews report that Janis's full model has not been supported in research, and only a few of its variables have been tested. [SECONDARY] — [Regent University journal](https://www.regent.edu/journal/emerging-leadership-journeys/groupthink-theory/)

**Drucker: organize dissent**
- Drucker, *The Effective Executive* (1967), as excerpted in *The Daily Drucker* (6 October entry): one should not make a decision unless there is disagreement. Drucker retells an Alfred Sloan story. When a GM committee seemed fully agreed, Sloan postponed the matter so that disagreement could develop and the group could understand the decision. A decision without an alternative is, for Drucker, a gambler's throw. [SECONDARY] — [O'Reilly, *The Daily Drucker*, 6 October](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/the-daily-drucker/9780061802669/6october.xhtml); [management.co.nz, "Organise Dissent"](https://management.co.nz/article/daily-drucker-organise-dissent)

**Commit after the decision**
- Netflix: dissent is gathered before the decision; everyone then "disagrees then commits". [PRIMARY-READ] — [Netflix culture memo](https://jobs.netflix.com/culture)
- Amazon: "disagree and commit"; decide with about 70 percent of the information; reversible decisions need a light process. [PRIMARY-READ] — [Amazon 2016 letter](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/company-news/2016-letter-to-shareholders)

**Time and continuity**
- SBB guide: each team member must give enough time to the work; members should stay for the whole effort; unit managers must free their staff to take part. The preparation programme also sets training needs for the team and contributors. [PRIMARY-READ] — [SBB guide, pp. 10–11](https://sgb.adalet.gov.tr/Resimler/SayfaDokuman/322020141843kamuidareleri.pdf)

### Inferences
- A participant contract in the product could cover: come with evidence; name alternatives; state dissent before the decision, openly or privately; then commit and carry out. Each item is backed by at least one source above. The bundle itself is my synthesis.
- Kahneman et al.'s checklist is written for the decision maker. In the product it fits the owner's review of the stratej's recommendation.

### Gaps
- No authority found on confidentiality duties of internal workshop participants (as opposed to consultants). Consultant codes cover confidentiality (section 6), but these were only partly verified.
- Lovallo and Sibony's exact list of process practices (for example explicit discussion of uncertainty, and views that contradict the senior leader) was not read in full.
- Janis's original text was not read.

## 6. Strategist or facilitator suitability

### Takeaway
The authorities describe a helper who is clear about role, builds trust and protects the client's own ability to decide. Schein separates three helping models and asks the consultant to switch between them consciously. Block puts weight on explicit contracting and on declining work with poor odds. Maister, Green and Galford's trust equation says self-orientation lowers trust. The IAF names six competency areas for facilitators. Consultant codes (CMC Canada, ICMCI) cover client interest and conflicts of interest. Mintzberg and Kahneman et al. add role boundaries: the planner supports, and the recommender stays separate from the decider.

### Cited Findings

**Schein: process consultation**
- Schein contrasts three helping models: the purchase-of-expertise model, the doctor–patient model, and process consultation. In the doctor–patient model, the expert diagnoses and prescribes, and the client carries out the fix. Process consultation builds a relationship that helps the client see and act on its own situation. It aims to raise the client's capacity to solve its own problems in the future. Choosing between the models is a real-time decision made throughout the work. [SECONDARY] — [SAGE Encyclopedia of Management Theory, "Process Consultation"](https://sk.sagepub.com/ency/edvol/embed/encyclopedia-of-management-theory/chpt/process-consultation); [MIT Sloan Management Review, Schein, "A General Philosophy of Helping: Process Consultation"](https://sloanreview.mit.edu/article/a-general-philosophy-of-helping-process-consultation/); [Thunderbird / ASU](https://thunderbird.asu.edu/thought-leadership/insights/which-model-business-consulting-best-suited-your-organization)

**Block: contracting and readiness**
- Block, *Flawless Consulting* (2nd ed. 2000; later editions exist). Contracting makes explicit what the client wants and offers, and what the consultant wants and offers. The book has sections on getting stuck on wants and offers, and on saying no. [SECONDARY, table of contents and summaries] — [O'Reilly, *Flawless Consulting* 2nd ed.](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/peter-block-flawless/9780787948030/); [Designed Learning, "Flawless Consulting and Saying No"](https://designedlearning.com/tag/flawless-consulting-2/)
- Secondary sources report two different "50/50" ideas. First, responsibility should be shared 50/50 between client and consultant. Second, a consultant should decline or postpone a project with less than a 50/50 chance of success. Some summaries mix these up. Check the contracting chapter before use. [SECONDARY] — [Designed Learning checklist PDF](https://designedlearning.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/Checklist3_23.pdf); [Designed Learning](https://designedlearning.com/tag/flawless-consulting-2/)
- Block treats resistance as common and often subtle. Effective implementation needs the client's internal commitment. [SECONDARY] — [Slideshare, Flawless Consulting notes](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/flawless-consulting-08292013/25759755)

**Maister, Green, Galford: trust**
- Maister, Green and Galford, *The Trusted Advisor* (Free Press, 2000). Trust = (Credibility + Reliability + Intimacy) / Self-orientation. Trust rises with the first three and falls as the advisor focuses on their own interests. [SECONDARY] — [Trusted Advisor Associates](https://www.trustedadvisor.com/books/the-trusted-advisor); [HRZone review](https://hrzone.com/book-review-the-trusted-advisor-by-david-maister-charles-green-and-robert-galford/)

**IAF core competencies**
- The International Association of Facilitators lists six areas: create collaborative client relationships; plan appropriate group processes; create and sustain a participatory environment; guide the group to appropriate and useful outcomes; build and maintain professional knowledge; model a positive professional attitude. These are the basis of the IAF Certified Professional Facilitator assessment. [PRIMARY-READ] — [IAF, The IAF Core Competencies](https://iaf-world.org/the-iaf-core-competencies/)
- The competencies were revised in 2021, per a search extract. The page I read shows only a 2003 date and a 2025 copyright. [SECONDARY for the revision date] — [IAF core competencies page](https://www.iaf-world.org/site/ar/professional/core-competencies)

**Consultant codes**
- CMC-Canada code: certified members must not review another member's work when they have a conflict of interest. The 2004 version said the client organization's overall interests come first in every assignment. [SECONDARY, search extracts of the code] — [CMC-Canada Code of Professional Conduct](https://cmc-canada.ca/codeofprofessionalconduct); [CMC-Canada code, 2004 version PDF](https://www.cmc-canada.ca/web/default/files/public/CMC-Canada%20Code%20of%20Pofessional%20Conduct%20(Version%202004%20).pdf)
- ICMCI (CMC-Global) published a draft Code of Responsible Use of Artificial Intelligence, dated May 2026. It builds on the ICMCI Code of Conduct. It applies to member institutes and all CMC holders. [SECONDARY, search extract] — [CMC-Global AI code PDF](https://www.cmc-global.org/sites/default/files/public/code_of_responsible_use_of_artificial_intelligence.pdf)

**Role boundaries**
- Mintzberg: planners act as finders, analysts and catalysts in support of strategy making. [SECONDARY] — [Buresund review](https://buresund.nu/books/the-rise-and-fall-of-strategic-planning)
- Kahneman et al.: a decider who shapes the recommendation loses the ability to judge it. [PRIMARY-READ] — [HBR 2011 via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21714386/)
- Johnson et al. (2010): the role of specialists is one of three ritual features that affect workshop outcomes. [ABSTRACT] — [Lancaster ePrints](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/45789)

### Inferences
- In the product, the stratej is closest to Schein's process consultant when helping the owner think, and to the expert model when proposing options. The sources suggest naming which mode is in use. This is my inference.
- The trust equation and the consultant codes both imply disclosing conflicts of interest. For example, the stratej should disclose any stake in a supplier, a software vendor or a follow-on project.
- The ICMCI AI code (May 2026 draft) is directly relevant to an AI-assisted stratej and should be read in full.

### Gaps
- The IAF Code of Ethics text (client service, conflict of interest, group autonomy, confidentiality) was not read. An unofficial copy exists, but I did not use it.
- The ICMCI Code of Conduct itself, and the IMC USA Code of Ethics (site unreachable), were not read. Exact competence clauses ("accept only work one is qualified for") are therefore not confirmed.
- Schein's primary books (*Process Consultation Revisited*, 1999; *Helping*, 2009; *Humble Consulting*, 2016) were not opened. His principles of helping are not listed here for that reason.
- No source found on how firms should choose an external strategist for a family SME (selection criteria, references, sector knowledge).

## 7. Client readiness and acceptance criteria

### Takeaway
Bryson asks for a quick readiness check and an "initial agreement", a plan for planning, before a full effort starts. The SBB guide makes top-manager ownership the indispensable condition and requires a preparation programme. Block expects explicit contracting. He also advises declining or postponing work with poor odds of success. Kahneman et al. reserve heavy decision review for important, recurring decisions. Amazon's 70 percent rule warns against waiting for perfect data. Carlock and Ward start family planning from shared family values. No authority found gives a scored readiness test for private family firms.

### Cited Findings

**Bryson**
- Bryson, *Strategic Planning for Public and Nonprofit Organizations* (Jossey-Bass/Wiley; several editions). Step 1 is an initial agreement among key decision makers and opinion leaders. It covers the purpose and worth of the effort, who should be involved, and the main tasks. Course material calls it the "plan for the plan". [SECONDARY, course slides and summaries] — [Slideshare, Bryson chapter 3](https://www.slideshare.net/robintgreene/bryson-chapter-3-initiating-and-agreeing-on-a-strategic-planning-process); [Shortform summary](https://www.shortform.com/summary/strategic-planning-for-public-and-nonprofit-organizations-summary-john-m-bryson)
- Initiators may first do a quick assessment of the organization's readiness. A good initial agreement names a task force or coordinating committee, secures resources and shows political support from key decision makers. A retreat is often the best way to reach it. Several successive agreements with a widening group may be needed. [SECONDARY] — [Slideshare, Bryson chapter 3](https://www.slideshare.net/robintgreene/bryson-chapter-3-initiating-and-agreeing-on-a-strategic-planning-process)
- Bryson's companion workbook (*Creating and Implementing Your Strategic Plan*) includes readiness worksheets and a model readiness questionnaire. [SECONDARY, table of contents] — [TextbookRush listing](https://www.textbookrush.com/browse/books/9780787967543)

**SBB guide (public sector, comparison)**
- Planning success depends largely on preparation before the plan. Preparation has three stages: ownership of the plan, organization of the process, and a preparation programme. [PRIMARY-READ] — [SBB guide, p. 8](https://sgb.adalet.gov.tr/Resimler/SayfaDokuman/322020141843kamuidareleri.pdf)
- Top-manager support and direction is an indispensable condition. The top manager should tell staff that they adopt the approach. [PRIMARY-READ] — [SBB guide, p. 8](https://sgb.adalet.gov.tr/Resimler/SayfaDokuman/322020141843kamuidareleri.pdf)
- The guide says the planning process itself must be planned. The preparation programme lists stages, activities, responsible people and units, a timetable, training needs and team members' names. The board finalizes it, and the top manager announces it inside the organization, including how changes will be made. [PRIMARY-READ] — [SBB guide, pp. 10–11](https://sgb.adalet.gov.tr/Resimler/SayfaDokuman/322020141843kamuidareleri.pdf)

**Block**
- Contracting makes the wants and offers of both sides explicit. Secondary sources report a rule to decline or postpone work with less than a 50/50 chance of success (see the conflict noted in section 6). [SECONDARY] — [Designed Learning](https://designedlearning.com/tag/flawless-consulting-2/); [O'Reilly, *Flawless Consulting*](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/peter-block-flawless/9780787948030/)

**Decision readiness**
- Kahneman et al.: formal decision quality control fits important, recurring decisions that justify a formal process. It is not for routine items. [PRIMARY-READ] — [HBR 2011 via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21714386/)
- Bezos: decide with about 70 percent of the desired information; reversible decisions need a light process. [PRIMARY-READ] — [Amazon 2016 letter](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/company-news/2016-letter-to-shareholders)
- Bain: a decision needs one named decider, or a pre-agreed rule if a group decides. [PRIMARY-READ] — [Bain, RAPID tool](https://www.bain.com/insights/rapid-tool-to-clarify-decision-accountability/)

**Family readiness**
- Carlock and Ward begin parallel planning with family values and business philosophy, then seek goals that support both. [SECONDARY] — [getAbstract summary](https://www.getabstract.com/en/summary/strategic-planning-for-the-family-business/2071); [FBCG resource page](https://www.thefbcg.com/resource/strategic-planning-for-the-family-business-parallel-planning-to-unify-the-family-and-business/)

### Inferences
- From these sources, a readiness check could ask:
  1. Is there one named decision owner?
  2. Has the owner said openly that they want the effort?
  3. Is time freed for team members?
  4. Is there a minimum of data (about "70 percent", not perfect)?
  5. Is there a bounded, important decision to work on?
  6. Have the family's values and the owners' wishes been discussed?
  Each question is tied to a source above. The list itself is my synthesis, not an authority's checklist.
- Bryson's "sequence of initial agreements" fits family firms. In practice, the owner may agree first, then the family, then unit leads.

### Gaps
- Bryson's actual readiness questions and the edition-specific wording were not read. The most recent edition and year were not confirmed this session.
- Block's exact readiness and decline criteria were seen only in secondary sources.
- Carlock and Ward's family readiness criteria (for example family commitment and agreement on values) could not be read in the original.
- No consultancy (McKinsey, BCG, Bain, Deloitte) readiness test for strategic planning engagements was found in public sources. Consultancy intake criteria appear to be proprietary.
- No source found on whether a firm in crisis should do strategic planning or deal with the crisis first.
