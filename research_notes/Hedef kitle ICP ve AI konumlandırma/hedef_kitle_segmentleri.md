# Target audience segments for a Turkey-origin strategy-planning SaaS, and which segment to enter first

Research date: 15 November 2026. Turkey data first, global data second. Older data is marked "(dated)". Every number below has a source. Items I could not verify are in the "Gaps" sections. The report writer should not promote gap items to facts.

## 1. How large is each segment in Turkey?

### Takeaway
Turkey has about 3.93 million SMEs (2024), which are 99.6% of all enterprises. Large firms are therefore only about 0.4%, roughly 14,000 to 18,000 firms (my inference). Turkish public bodies have a legal duty to make strategic plans. The startup segment is small in money terms: 306 funding rounds and 589 million USD in 2025.

### Cited Findings
**Enterprises by size (TÜİK 2024, published 24 December 2025)**
- There are 3,928,000 SMEs in industry and services. SMEs are 99.6% of all enterprises. — [GazeteABC, reporting TÜİK KOBİ İstatistikleri 2024](https://www.gazeteabc.com/kobiler-ekonominin-can-damari-2024te-3-9-milyon-girisim-faaliyette/598945/)
- SMEs hold 68.5% of employment and 44.1% of turnover. They also hold 41.2% of value added and 29.6% of exports. — [GazeteABC / TÜİK 2024](https://www.gazeteabc.com/kobiler-ekonominin-can-damari-2024te-3-9-milyon-girisim-faaliyette/598945/)
- Value added per employee rises with firm size. Medium firms make 969 thousand TL, small firms 558 thousand TL and micro firms 175 thousand TL. — [GazeteABC / TÜİK 2024](https://www.gazeteabc.com/kobiler-ekonominin-can-damari-2024te-3-9-milyon-girisim-faaliyette/598945/)
- SMEs by sector: trade 35.1%, transport and storage 15.3%, manufacturing 12.1%. — [GazeteABC / TÜİK 2024](https://www.gazeteabc.com/kobiler-ekonominin-can-damari-2024te-3-9-milyon-girisim-faaliyette/598945/)
- TÜİK size classes count employees only: micro 1–9, small 10–49, medium 50–249, large 250+. — [Alomaliye, TÜİK dış ticaret istatistikleri 2024](https://www.alomaliye.com/2025/09/26/girisim-ozelliklerine-gore-dis-ticaret-istatistikleri-2024/)
- Export shares by size class in 2024: micro 2.4%, small 10.7%, medium 16.5%, large 70.4%. — [Alomaliye / TÜİK 2024](https://www.alomaliye.com/2025/09/26/girisim-ozelliklerine-gore-dis-ticaret-istatistikleri-2024/)
- The legal KOBİ definition changed in August 2025. An SME now has fewer than 250 employees and up to 1 billion TL of turnover or balance sheet. The old limit was 500 million TL. — [erp-software.org, Turkey ERP market](https://erp-software.org/en/erp-worldwide/turkey/)
- A frequently cited figure concerns gazelle and high-growth firms, not all firms. These SMEs rose from 20,715 to 29,882 in five years. High-growth large firms rose from 1,937 to 2,680. (2023 data) — [Bloomberg HT](https://www.bloomberght.com/ceylan-ve-hizli-buyuyen-girisim-sayisi-5-yilda-yuzde-40-tan-fazla-artti-3737826)

I could not extract text from the PDF. — [KOSGEB, Türkiye'deki KOBİ'lere İlişkin Bazı İstatistiki Göstergeler (2026)](https://webdosya.kosgeb.gov.tr/Content/Upload/Dosya/Kurumsal/2026/Tu%CC%88rkiye'deki_KOBI%CC%87'lere_I%CC%87lis%CC%A7kin_Baz%C4%B1_I%CC%87statistiki_Go%CC%88stergeler.pdf)

**Family businesses**
- Family firms are about 95% of Turkish companies. This figure comes from a 2017 estimate (dated, secondary). — [erp-software.org, citing Sabancı/TÜİK/TAİDER](https://erp-software.org/en/erp-worldwide/turkey/); [DergiPark article citing Corrado et al. 2017](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/4358788)

- A survey of 200 family firms found that only 50% have institutionalised. — [Para Dergi (July 2023), Ortak Akıl Danışmanlık survey](https://www.paradergi.com.tr/is-dunyasi-kulis/2023/07/24/aile-sirketlerinin-yalnizca-yarisi-kurumsallasiyor)

**Startups and founders**

- A blog says the startups.watch database tracks "over 20,000" Turkish and Turkish-founded startups. This is unverified and secondary. — [blog.mean.ceo, November 2026](https://blog.mean.ceo/startups-turkey-news-november-2026/)

**Public institutions with a legal duty to plan**
- Law 5018 sets a strategic planning duty for public administrations in general. Law 5393, article 41, applies it to municipalities with more than 50,000 people. Law 5302 covers special provincial administrations. Law 5216 covers metropolitan municipalities. Municipalities first prepared plans after the 2006 local elections. — [DergiPark article on municipal strategic planning](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/1129249); [5393 Belediye Kanunu text](https://www.kagithane.istanbul/images/pdf/5393-Sayili-Belediye-Kanunu.pdf)
- Current municipality structure: 30 metropolitan, 51 provincial, 519 metropolitan-district, 403 district and 399 town municipalities. There are also 33 affiliated bodies with legal personality. — [Alomaliye, belediyelerin 2024 mali durumu (October 2025)](https://www.alomaliye.com/2025/10/03/belediyelerimizin-2024-yili-faaliyetlerinin-ve-mali-durumlarinin-onceki-yillarla-karsilastirmali-degerlendirilmesi/amp/)
- By regulation, each administration's own staff prepare its strategic plan. SBB writes evaluation reports on draft plans and monitors them. — [SBB Teşkilat Görev Yönergesi (2023)](https://www.sbb.gov.tr/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/SBB-Teskilat-Gorev-Yonergesi_10032023.pdf); [Yönetmelik text (Gazi Üniversitesi copy)](https://webupload.gazi.edu.tr/upload/1060/2024/3/12/27803f91-faf3-4aa6-9c0c-2509f0ecd788-yonetmelIk.pdf)
- SBB runs its own monitoring system, the Cumhurbaşkanlığı Plan Program İzleme Değerlendirme Sistemi (CPPİDS). — [SBB](https://www.sbb.gov.tr/?p=64086)

### Inferences
- Large firms (250+) number about 14,000 to 18,000. Derivation: 3.928M SMEs at 99.6% gives about 3.944M enterprises. The rest, 0.4%, is about 15,800. Rounding of "99.6%" widens the range. Check this against the TÜİK table.
- The mid-market (50–1000 employees) is likely a few tens of thousands of firms. That makes it a real but countable list, suitable for named-account sales.
- Each year about 1,000 Turkish startups or fewer raise outside money. The funded-founder segment is therefore small. Unfunded solo founders are many but hard to count.
- Family ownership is the normal case in Turkish SMEs and mid-market firms. So the "C-level mid-market" segment and the "family business moving to professional management" segment largely overlap in Turkey.
- The public segment has a legal trigger: every plan cycle is usually 5 years. Yet own staff must write the plan, and SBB already runs CPPİDS. A SaaS tool would compete with or complement the state system, so it must fit SBB templates.

### Gaps
- I did not find the exact TÜİK 2024 count of medium (50–249) and large (250+) firms. The KOSGEB PDF has them, but I could not extract the text.
- I did not find an official count of public administrations obliged to plan under Law 5018. That count covers ministries, agencies, universities and municipalities over 50,000 people. The number of municipalities over 50,000 people is also missing.
- I found no official startups.watch count of active startups or founders.
- I found no recent primary-source share of family firms. The 90–95% figure is dated and secondary.
- I found no data on NGO and foundation counts, or on how many holdings run formal strategy cycles.

## 2. How often does each segment actually do formal strategic planning?

### Takeaway
Strategic planning is the most-used management tool among large-company managers worldwide. Its usage, however, fell from 88% in 2006 to 48% in 2017. For SMEs and founders, formal planning is common but not universal. Its link to performance is positive but small, and it depends on context.

### Cited Findings
- Bain's 2017 survey of 1,268 managers ranked Strategic Planning first by global usage, used by 48% of respondents. Usage was 88% in 2006. — [Bain, Management Tools & Trends 2017](https://www.bain.com/insights/management-tools-and-trends-2017/)
- Mission and Vision Statements fell to 32% usage from 88% in 1993. Scenario and Contingency Planning fell to 19% from a 70% peak. — [Bain 2017](https://www.bain.com/insights/management-tools-and-trends-2017/)
- Bain says satisfaction with one tool "can vary widely depending on company size and region". Asia-Pacific managers use on average 12.2 tools, nearly twice North America's average. Emerging-market managers use more tools. — [Bain 2017](https://www.bain.com/insights/management-tools-and-trends-2017/)
- Strategic planning had the highest satisfaction of all 25 tools in the 2011 survey. Bain finds a positive link between usage and satisfaction in general. (dated) — [Consultant's Mind summary of Bain](https://www.consultantsmind.com/2012/11/14/bain-management-tools-and-trends/); [Bain, Strategic Planning tool page](https://www.bain.com/es-ar/insights/management-tools-strategic-planning/)
- A meta-analysis of 14 studies found a positive and significant link between formal planning and small-firm financial performance. The effect per study is small. Moderating variables explain much of the variation. (Schwenk and Shrader 1993, dated) — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/sae/entthe/v17y1993i3p53-64.html)
- A 2025 UK Enterprise Research Centre paper says small business planning is "sticky", meaning firms that plan keep planning. It also says planning is not universal. In its analysis, one group of older, export-oriented, productive firms does not plan formally, and non-planners sometimes outperform. — [ERC Research Paper 121 (November 2025)](https://www.enterpriseresearch.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/ERC-RP-121.-Small-Business-Planning-is-Sticky-but-neither-a-Universal-Practice-nor-a-Guaranteed-Path-to-Success-final.pdf) (headline findings via search summary, PDF text not extractable)
- O'Regan and Ghobadian (2002) found that SMEs with formal strategic planning face fewer barriers when they implement strategy. — [Emerald, Management Decision](https://www.emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/00251740210438490/full/html)
- A South African study of 73 SMEs found that most lacked formal, documented planning. It found a small negative link to financial performance. — [Wits University thesis](https://wiredspace.wits.ac.za/bitstreams/ca336a9c-e74e-4f97-a799-a1de5a8bc669/download)
- Greene and Hopp (2017) studied 1,088 founders. Founders who write a formal plan are 16% more likely to reach viability than similar non-planners. Founders seeking outside finance are 19% more likely to write a plan. Planning helps more when challenges are greater. The authors say writing a plan is not the best strategy for every founder. — [Irish Times reprint of HBR article](https://www.irishtimes.com/business/work/entrepreneurs-who-write-formal-business-plans-are-16-more-likely-to-achieve-viability-1.3159045); [Strategic Entrepreneurship Journal via zendy](https://zendy.io/title/10.1002/sej.1245)
- Better-educated founders, innovators and founders with complex products plan more often. — [FE News summary of Greene and Hopp](https://fenews.co.uk/?p=14536)
- In Turkey, a 2026 study of 601 SME managers says digital transformation slows mainly because of tension between generations. This tension "locks" decision making. Technology spending is not the main cause. — [Forbes Türkiye, 4 February 2026](https://www.forbes.com.tr/dergi/kurucular-temkinli-ikinci-kusak-hizli-dijital-donusum-gerilim-yaratiyor)
- Turkish public bodies must plan by law. Plans follow SBB guidance and SBB evaluates the drafts. — [SBB yönerge](https://www.sbb.gov.tr/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/SBB-Teskilat-Gorev-Yonergesi_10032023.pdf)

### Inferences
- Large and mid-market firms already have a strategy habit, often as an annual budget cycle. Their pain is likely in execution and review, not in "doing strategy for the first time". The product's execution module (targets, initiatives, reviews) fits this segment.
- Founders plan mostly when an outside party asks. Examples are an investor, a grant (KOSGEB, TÜBİTAK) or an accelerator. Founder demand is therefore event-driven and short-lived, which raises churn risk.
- In Turkish family firms, the second generation pushing professional management is a likely trigger to buy. The Forbes 2026 finding suggests that the buyer and the blocker can be different family members.
- Public bodies have the most regular and forced planning cycle. Their pain is compliance and monitoring work, not strategic thinking.

### Gaps
- I found no Turkish survey on how many SMEs or mid-market firms have a written strategic plan.
- I could not open the newer Bain surveys (2020s). The 2017 numbers are the latest I verified.
- I could not verify the well-known Brinckmann, Grichnik and Kapsa (2010) meta-analysis on planning in new versus established small firms in this session.
- I found no McKinsey or HBR prevalence survey specific to strategy execution software in this session.

## 3. Willingness to pay, budget owner and price sensitivity

### Takeaway
Turkish software buyers face high inflation and a falling lira. Local vendors reset TL price lists every year. Paid cloud use grows with firm size: 17.3% of small firms, 31.8% of medium firms and 54.3% of large firms buy cloud services. Readiness to pay for SaaS is clearly highest in medium and large firms.

### Cited Findings
- In 2025, 20.4% of Turkish firms with 10 or more employees bought paid cloud services, up from 16.4% in 2023. By size: small 17.3%, medium 31.8%, large 54.3%. — [erp-software.org citing TÜİK ICT usage survey 2025](https://erp-software.org/en/erp-worldwide/turkey/)
- ERP use: 23.6% of firms with 10–49 employees, 46.0% of medium firms and 76.5% of large firms (TÜİK, year 2024 or 2025). — [erp-software.org](https://erp-software.org/en/erp-worldwide/turkey/)
- Logo resets its TL price list each year on 7 January, so price lists last 12 months. Tiger 3 Enterprise costs 817,900 TL for one user (2026 list, before KDV). — [erp-software.org](https://erp-software.org/en/erp-worldwide/turkey/)
- Global vendors price in USD or EUR in Turkey. Microsoft Business Central Essentials costs 80 USD per user per month. Odoo starts at 19.90 EUR per user per month. — [erp-software.org](https://erp-software.org/en/erp-worldwide/turkey/)
- The dollar rose from 42.85 TL (31 December 2025) to 48.21 TL (2 September 2026). That is about a 12.5% loss for the lira in eight months. Logo budgets 23% inflation for end-2026. — [erp-software.org](https://erp-software.org/en/erp-worldwide/turkey/)
- 86% of Logo's billed sales were recurring revenue in 2025. Cloud and SaaS were 52% of Logo revenue in 2025 and 59% in the first half of 2026. — [erp-software.org](https://erp-software.org/en/erp-worldwide/turkey/)
- KOSGEB gives interest-free loans of 1 to 20 million TL for digital transformation. Consulting and training are excluded. — [erp-software.org](https://erp-software.org/en/erp-worldwide/turkey/)
- Tax advisers strongly shape SME software choices. Buyers often deal with three parties: the vendor, the integrator and the tax adviser. Post-dated cheques are normal in B2B trade. — [erp-software.org](https://erp-software.org/en/erp-worldwide/turkey/)
- PwC Strategy& expects the Turkish SME business-operations software market to grow 28% between 2020 and 2026. — [PwC Strategy& Turkey](https://www.strategyand.pwc.com/tr/en/medya/kobi-is-operasyonlari-yazilimi-pazar-ve-rekabet-ortami-raporu.html)
- In Turkish public bodies, the strategy development unit owns the plan. SBB evaluates drafts. Staff must prepare the plan themselves. — [Yönetmelik text](https://webupload.gazi.edu.tr/upload/1060/2024/3/12/27803f91-faf3-4aa6-9c0c-2509f0ecd788-yonetmelIk.pdf)

### Inferences
- Price in TL with yearly indexing, or in USD with a TL display. Annual prepayment discounts are a common local pattern.
- The budget owner in mid-market and family firms is likely the owner or CEO, or the general manager in professionalising firms. The CFO may own the budget cycle. In public bodies, the Strateji Geliştirme Daire Başkanlığı owns it, and buying follows public procurement rules (Law 4734).
- Solo founders are the most price-sensitive group. They also churn after an event such as a funding round, which supports a low-price or free self-serve tier.
- KOSGEB funds exclude consulting. A tool sold as "software" may fit digital-transformation support better than a consulting package. This needs checking against KOSGEB programme rules.

### Gaps
- I found no data on what Turkish firms spend on strategy consulting each year, and no consulting day rates.
- I found no Turkish survey on SME willingness to pay for SaaS under inflation.
- I found no price data for strategy-execution tools (for example Cascade, Quantive, Profit.co) in Turkey.
- Public procurement thresholds and the buying cycle of municipalities were not researched in this session.

## 4. Consultants as a channel, and NGOs, universities and holdings

### Takeaway
Little direct evidence came up in this session. The public-sector rule that "own staff prepare the plan" may limit consultants' role there. That same rule creates room for a guided tool that staff use themselves.

### Cited Findings
- Public strategic plans must be prepared by the administration and its own staff. — [Yönetmelik](https://webupload.gazi.edu.tr/upload/1060/2024/3/12/27803f91-faf3-4aa6-9c0c-2509f0ecd788-yonetmelIk.pdf)
- Universities in Turkey prepare strategic plans, and SBB evaluates the drafts. One example is a 2024–2028 draft plan evaluation for a university. — [SBB search result, hakkari.edu.tr document](https://hakkari.edu.tr/Document/18/18-ab52341e-afd3-47e6-bafe-252ec24d29a5.pdf)
- KOSGEB digital-transformation loans exclude consulting and training. This weakens consulting-only offers to SMEs. — [erp-software.org](https://erp-software.org/en/erp-worldwide/turkey/)

### Inferences
- Consultants are likely a partner channel, not the first paying segment. They could resell or white-label the tool to family firms and mid-market clients. Multi-tenant design supports a "consultant workspace with many client tenants" model.
- Universities are a public sub-segment with the same legal plan cycle and SBB format. They are likely easier to reach than municipalities because they are fewer and more homogeneous.

### Gaps
- I found no count of Turkish management consultancies and no market size for Turkish consulting.
- I found no evidence on NGO or holding-company planning practice in Turkey.

## 5. Beachhead logic: one segment first, or all at once?

### Takeaway
Beachhead frameworks say: pick one homogeneous, reachable segment and win it first. Bottom-up companies such as Notion and Miro started with individuals and added enterprise sales later, not at the same time. One critic says no company succeeded with both motions at once. Notion and Miro partly contradict this, so the claim is contested.

### Cited Findings
- Disciplined Entrepreneurship (Aulet) asks the same questions for segment and beachhead choice. Do customers have money and a reason to spend it? Can you reach them and beat the competition? What adjacent markets does the segment open? Are you interested in it? — [O'Reilly, Disciplined Entrepreneurship ch. 2](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/disciplined-entrepreneurship-24/9781118692288/11_chap02.html)
- The beachhead is the easiest segment to capture, not the biggest. It must be homogeneous: users share a product, a sales approach and word of mouth. — [Rework, beachhead market](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/startup-terms/beachhead-market); [Simone Smerilli summary](https://www.simonesmerilli.com/business/disciplined-entrepreneurship)
- One summary gives a beachhead market size of roughly 20–100 million USD per year and recommends bottom-up counts. — [tianpan.co summary](https://tianpan.co/blog/2025/06/14/disciplined-entrepreneurship)
- Aulet's process starts from 6–12 candidate segments and picks one. Winning a dominant share there gives the strength to enter adjacent markets. — [MIT Sloan, Disciplined Entrepreneurship](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/ideas-made-to-matter/disciplined-entrepreneurship-6-questions-startup-success); [MIT Executive Education](https://executive.mit.edu/launching-a-successful-start-up-3-the-beachhead-market-MC7FUMDZ6IU5AIPP4WGIPN2PZJI4.html)
- Notion grew bottom-up. It won students and individual creators first, then teams. A useful free tier fed a Free, Plus, Business, Enterprise ladder. Enterprise monetisation is sales-led. (secondary analysis) — [Aakash Gupta, How Notion Grows](https://www.news.aakashg.com/p/how-notion-grows); [upgrowth teardown](https://upgrowth.in/notion-built-10b-company-gtm-strategy-teardown/)
- Miro combined product-led growth with sales-assisted enterprise deals. It turned free usage into enterprise deployments. (secondary) — [Aakash Gupta, How Miro Grows](https://www.news.aakashg.com/p/how-miro-grows-tactical-lessons-from); [Sacra](https://sacra.com/chat/h/41709844-438e-46a9-a009-5c004e2da936/)
- Building features for enterprise can harm the self-serve channel. One source names this risk for Atlassian and similar firms. One critic argues no company succeeded with both strategies at the same time. In practice, bottom-up growth came first and enterprise sales followed. — [But First Value](https://butfirstvalue.substack.com/p/6-truths-and-3-signals-of-growth); [Angular Ventures](https://medium.com/angularventures/how-the-no-code-design-paradigm-is-revolutionizing-enterprise-software-e2fe3f5807ae)

### Inferences
- Applying Aulet's questions to the data above:
  - (a) Mid-market C-level and (b) family firms moving to professional management overlap in Turkey. Both have money, a countable list and a planning habit, and more medium and large firms already buy cloud software. This combined segment fits best for an "execution and review" value proposition.
  - (c) Solo founders are many, cheap to reach online and event-driven, with low willingness to pay. They suit a free or low-price self-serve tier that feeds the funnel. As the first revenue segment they look weak.
  - (d) Consultants fit as a channel, used in parallel, not as the beachhead.
  - (e) Public bodies have a forced, repeating cycle and fixed SBB formats. They also bring public procurement, an existing state system (CPPİDS) and long sales cycles. They suit a later, separate offer, possibly starting with universities.
- One codebase can serve several segments. One go-to-market motion and one message probably cannot serve all of them at once. The evidence points to "one paid beachhead plus one free self-serve funnel", not equal focus on every segment.
- Notion and Miro became enterprise tools through daily, team-wide use. Strategy work is periodic, so bottom-up spread from founders to enterprises may be weaker for this product. This is my inference, not a sourced finding.

### Gaps
- I did not fetch Geoffrey Moore's "Crossing the Chasm" beachhead text in this session. The usual summary ("win one niche, use it as a bowling pin") is from general knowledge and is not verified here.
- I could not verify the exact wording and count of Aulet's segment criteria. Sources differ (6 questions versus a longer list).
- I found no case study of a strategy-planning SaaS (for example Cascade, Quantive or Spider Impact) that names its first segment.
- I found no Turkish B2B SaaS case that expanded from SMEs to enterprises or from founders to enterprises.
