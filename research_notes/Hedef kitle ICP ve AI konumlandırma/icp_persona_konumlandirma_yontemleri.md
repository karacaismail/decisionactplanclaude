# Target audience, ICP, buyer personas, and product and brand positioning in B2B SaaS

Scope: definitions, differences, step-by-step methods, templates and common mistakes for a Turkey-origin strategy-planning SaaS (pre-strategy preparation, strategy formulation, strategy execution). Researched 15 November 2026. Source quality is marked inline. "Secondary" means a blog or vendor page that attributes a claim to an original author or firm. That attribution was not checked against the original in this session.

## Definitions and differences: target market, target audience, segment, ICP, buyer persona vs user persona, buying committee, JTBD

### Takeaway
The terms form a funnel of scope. Market and segment describe groups of companies. The ICP is the account-level profile of the companies that buy, stay and expand. Personas describe the individual people inside those accounts and how they decide. The buying committee is the set of personas who decide together. JTBD describes the progress the buyer is trying to make, independent of who they are. Gartner data shows B2B decisions are group decisions with frequent conflict, so single-persona thinking is not enough.

### Cited Findings
**ICP (account level) vs buyer persona (person level)**
- ICP is commonly defined as account-level fit built from firmographics (industry, size, revenue, location), technographics (tech stack) and behavioral signals (buying intent, trigger events) that predict whether a deal will close, expand and renew — [ZoomInfo (vendor)](https://pipeline.zoominfo.com/sales/ideal-client-profile)
- Practitioner convention: the ICP defines company-level traits, while the buyer persona zooms in on the individual decision-makers inside those companies; B2B teams need both — [Huble (secondary)](https://huble.com/blog/what-is-a-buyer-persona). Note: this split is a common convention, not a formal standard.
- One practitioner definition centers the ICP on the accounts that stay longest and expand, and says to revisit it as product and market change — [Growthspree (vendor)](https://www.growthspreeofficial.com/blogs/icp-definition-b2b-saas)
- HubSpot's widely quoted definition: a buyer persona is a "semi-fictional representation" of the ideal customer, based on real data, interviews and some educated assumptions — attributed by [Huble (secondary)](https://huble.com/blog/what-is-a-buyer-persona); HubSpot's own lesson frames it as a definition of the ideal buyer written as if about a specific person — [HubSpot Academy](https://academy.hubspot.com/lessons/buyer-personas). HubSpot's current definition page was not fetched directly.
- Buyer Persona Institute (Adele Revella) position: personas must be "built from the real words of real buyers," using interviews with buyers who recently made the same decision. Persona content covers attitudes, concerns, decision criteria and the journey, including whether buyers chose you, a competitor or the status quo — [Buyer Persona Institute](https://www.buyerpersona.com/)
- Contrast with HubSpot: BPI's stance excludes "educated assumptions" as the core basis, while HubSpot's definition allows them. This is a real methodological difference — [Buyer Persona Institute](https://www.buyerpersona.com/) vs [HubSpot Academy](https://academy.hubspot.com/lessons/buyer-personas)

**Buying committee (buying group / buying center)**
- Gartner (May 2025): B2B buying groups range from 5 to 16 people across as many as four functions; 74% of buyer teams show "unhealthy conflict" during the decision; groups that reach consensus are 2.5 times more likely to report a high-quality deal. Survey of 632 buyers, August to September 2024 — [Gartner press release, 7 May 2025](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-05-07-gartner-sales-survey-finds-74-percent-of-b2b-buyer-teams-demonstrate-unhealthy-conflict-during-the-decision-process?authuser=0) (seen via search snippet; the direct fetch returned 403)
- Gartner (June 2025): 61% of B2B buyers prefer an overall rep-free buying experience — [Gartner press release, 25 June 2025](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-sales-survey-finds-61-percent-of-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-buying-experience?afsrc=1)
- A later Gartner survey (645 buyers, August to September 2025) reports 67% prefer a rep-free experience, and 69% prefer to validate AI-generated insights with sales reps — reported by [Barchart/press wire](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/2038403/gartner-survey-finds-69-of-b2b-buyers-turn-to-sales-reps-to-validate-ai-generated-insights) and [Demand Gen Report](https://www.demandgenreport.com/?p=53046). The 61% and 67% figures come from different surveys; do not merge them.
- Older Gartner figures that are still widely repeated: a buying group of 6 to 10 decision makers, and buyers spending about 17% of buying time meeting potential suppliers. Gartner's framework lists six "buying jobs" (problem identification, solution exploration, requirements building, supplier selection, validation, consensus creation) that happen in no fixed order — [Growth Method (secondary)](https://growthmethod.com/gartner-b2b-buying-journey/); [Boomerang glossary (secondary)](https://getboomerang.ai/glossaries/6-buying-jobs-b2b-deal-gartner). Dating conflicts: some blogs cite the 2019 "B2B Buying Journey", others a 2025 report. Demand Gen Report has a piece warning teams to "check the date on your buying group stats" — [Demand Gen Report](https://www.demandgenreport.com/?p=54785). Gartner's own page returned 403.

**Jobs-to-be-done (JTBD)**
- Christensen et al. define a job as the progress a person is trying to make in a particular circumstance; the milkshake study found morning buyers "hired" the shake to make a long, boring commute tolerable — HBR, September 2016, "Know Your Customers' Jobs to Be Done" (reprint R1609D), summarized at [Innosight](https://www.innosight.com/?p=19201) and [HBS Working Knowledge](https://hbswk.hbs.edu/item/6496.html). Full HBR text was not fetched. Classic.
- Moesta and Spiek: the "struggling moment" is where a switch starts; switch interviews talk to people shortly after they switched; the Four Forces of Progress (push of the situation, pull of the new solution, anxiety of the new, habit of the present) explain whether a switch happens — [jobstobedone.org: the struggling moment](https://jobstobedone.org/the-struggling-moment/); [jobstobedone.org: the four forces](https://jobstobedone.org/the-four-forces/); [Bob Moesta, Business of Software talk](https://businessofsoftware.org/talks/understanding-your-customer-jtbd/)
- The job-story format "When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [expected outcome]" is attributed to Alan Klement and Intercom; the Christensen/Moesta school treats a job as desired progress in a circumstance, while Tony Ulwick's school treats it as a functional process — [LogRocket (secondary)](https://blog.logrocket.com/ux-design/how-ux-teams-can-use-jtbd-framework/); [Mind the Product](https://www.mindtheproduct.com/jobs-to-be-done-for-product-managers/)

### Inferences
- A working vocabulary for the founder (inference, built from the sources above):
  - Target market: all organizations that could in principle buy strategy-planning software.
  - Segment: a group inside the market with shared needs and buying behavior (for example, family-owned Turkish mid-market firms preparing a first formal strategy).
  - Target audience: the segment(s) the company chooses to address with marketing now. In B2B this is a mix of accounts and people.
  - ICP: the account profile with the best fit, which closes fastest, pays, stays and expands.
  - Buyer persona: an evidence-based description of how a role inside an ICP account decides (the champion, the economic buyer, a blocker).
  - User persona: a role who uses the product daily (for example a strategy office analyst or a department head who updates KPIs). User personas drive UX. Buyer personas drive marketing and sales.
  - Buying committee: the set of buyer and user roles who decide together.
  - JTBD: the progress the account is trying to make (for example "turn the board's growth ambition into a plan the managers actually execute").
- For a strategy-planning product, the buyer and user are very likely different people. The CEO or board may sign; a strategy office, CFO or planning manager may champion; department managers use it during execution. This makes the buyer-vs-user split essential, not optional.
- Gartner's conflict data suggests the product and sales material should help the buying group reach consensus. A strategy-planning tool may naturally do this, because alignment is part of its job.

## How to build an ICP from evidence, including before having customers

### Takeaway
Build the ICP from your best existing accounts (win rate, time to close, retention, expansion), then add trigger events, pain intensity and ability to pay, and write an explicit negative ICP. Before customers exist, write a hypothesis ICP and test it through Mom Test style interviews about past behavior. Firmographics alone are a weak predictor.

### Cited Findings
- ICP layers: firmographics, technographics, and behavioral signals such as intent and trigger events — [ZoomInfo (vendor)](https://pipeline.zoominfo.com/sales/ideal-client-profile)
- Firmographics alone do not define an ICP; add technographics, intent and growth signals — [Landbase (vendor)](https://www.landbase.com/blog/why-firmographics-alone-dont-define-icp)
- Trigger events three to six months before purchase, exact pain statements from call recordings, alternatives considered and recurring objections are more predictive than firmographics alone; example scorecard weights trigger fit (funding, hiring, growth events, compliance change) at 25%; it recommends back-testing scorecards against historical CRM data — [Saas Hero (agency)](https://www.saashero.net/strategy/b2b-saas-target-market-personas/)
- Negative ICP: write explicit bad-fit filters that disqualify accounts before they use sales time — [Kustiq (vendor)](https://kustiq.com/blog/how-to-build-b2b-ideal-customer-profile). An unverified case study claims negative scoring cut lead volume 40% and lifted win rate 22% — [Saas Hero (agency)](https://www.saashero.net/strategy/b2b-saas-target-market-personas/). A vendor rule of thumb says an ICP should disqualify about 70% of inbound — [Prospeo (vendor)](https://prospeo.io/s/ideal-customer-criteria). Treat both numbers as anecdotes, not benchmarks.
- Specificity example: "any company with sales teams" is too broad; "B2B SaaS firms with SDRs struggling to convert inbound leads" is usable — [Userpilot](https://userpilot.com/blog/?p=354756)
- Dunford's positioning method treats best-fit customers as the customers who care most about your differentiated value, and the traits that make them care. Best-fit is derived after alternatives, differentiation and value, not before — [April Dunford, quickstart guide](https://www.aprildunford.com/post/a-quickstart-guide-to-positioning)
- The Mom Test (Rob Fitzpatrick) has three rules for honest customer conversations: talk about their life, not your idea; ask about specifics in the past, not opinions about the future; talk less and listen more — [Shortform summary](https://www.shortform.com/blog/what-is-the-mom-test/); [Unusual Ventures](https://www.unusual.vc/rob-fitzpatricks-mom-test/). The book is a classic (2013); summaries were used, not the book.
- Switch interviews (Moesta/Spiek) interview people shortly after they switched solutions, to find the struggling moment and the four forces — [jobstobedone.org](https://jobstobedone.org/the-struggling-moment/)

### Inferences
Step-by-step ICP method (synthesized from the sources above):
1. List the candidate segments. Write a one-line hypothesis ICP for each one. Example: "Turkish companies with 100 to 1,000 employees whose board asked for a 3-year strategy this year."
2. Write the trigger events for each segment. Examples: a new CEO, a generational handover in a family firm, a funding round, a new regulation, an IPO plan, or a failed annual plan.
3. Estimate pain intensity. Ask what they use today (spreadsheets, PowerPoint, consultants, nothing) and what that costs them.
4. Check ability and authority to pay. Who owns a budget line for strategy work today? Consultant fees are a strong signal.
5. Run 15 to 30 Mom Test interviews across the candidate segments. Ask about the last strategy cycle, not about the product idea.
6. Score each segment on trigger frequency, pain, budget, reachability and fit with your differentiated value.
7. Pick one beachhead segment. Write the negative ICP. Examples: companies with no budget owner, or companies that only want a free template.
8. After the first 10 to 20 paying customers, rebuild the ICP from data. Use win rate, sales cycle length, activation, retention and expansion.
- Note: the interview count in step 5 is my inference. The sources did not give a pre-customer interview number.
- For this product, a strong pre-customer budget signal is existing spend on strategy consultants or strategy workshops. The JTBD "competing alternative" is probably consultants plus spreadsheets. This connects the ICP to Dunford's competitive alternatives step.

## How to build personas from interviews and avoid fictional personas

### Takeaway
Interview recent buyers, including people who chose a competitor or did nothing. Organize findings by Revella's 5 Rings of Buying Insight. Typical guidance is about 8 to 15 interviews per persona until themes repeat. In complex B2B deals, Dunford argues that a detailed persona for the champion matters most; other committee roles need objection handling, not full personas.

### Cited Findings
- Revella's 5 Rings of Buying Insight (trademark of Buyer Persona Institute): Priority Initiatives, Success Factors, Perceived Barriers, Decision Criteria, Buyer's Journey. The first four inform content and messaging; the fifth guides who to address, how and where — [SoftGuide (secondary)](https://www.softguide.com/software/further-sales/articles/5-rings-of-buying-insights-1); [Buyer Persona Institute research page](https://buyerpersona.com/buyer-persona-research)
- Common definitions of the rings (from practitioner summaries of Revella's book): Priority Initiatives is what triggers the buyer to invest now; Success Factors is the results the buyer expects; Perceived Barriers is what makes the buyer doubt you or the approach; Decision Criteria is the attributes used to compare options; Buyer's Journey is the steps, people and sources involved — [CustomerThink](https://customerthink.com/unlocking-modern-buyer-personas-5-key-buyer-insights-to-supercharge-your-marketing-sales/); [MarketingProfs 2024](https://www.marketingprofs.com/articles/2024/51630/modern-buyer-personas-for-high-consideration-purchases). The exact BPI wording was not verified.
- Interview method: unscripted, journalist-style interviews with people who recently evaluated a solution like yours. BPI defines a recent buyer as someone who made the exact same decision, ideally in the last 3 to 6 months; another BPI page says typically the last 12 months — [BPI blog](https://buyerpersona.com/blog/interviewing-recent-buyers-enhances-buyer-persona-authenticity); [BPI research page](https://buyerpersona.com/buyer-persona-research)
- Interview count: secondary guidance says 8 to 12 per persona, with themes repeating around the 10th interview; another says 8 to 15 per persona hypothesis until patterns stop changing; another says about 12 — [FullEnrich](https://fullenrich.com/content/b2b-buyer-persona-research); [Prospeo](https://prospeo.io/s/buyer-persona-research); [Saas Hero](https://www.saashero.net/strategy/b2b-saas-buyer-personas-gtm/). No count was confirmed on buyerpersona.com itself.
- Dunford: building a persona for every stakeholder in an enterprise deal was mostly unhelpful. The one persona that mattered was the champion, who leads the effort, shortlists vendors and builds buy-in. Her line: "We need positioning for the champion and good objection handling for everyone else." — [April Dunford, multi-segment guide](https://aprildunford.substack.com/p/a-guide-to-positioning-with-multiple)
- Persona research should include buyers who chose a competitor or the status quo, not only your winners — [Buyer Persona Institute](https://www.buyerpersona.com/)

### Inferences
Persona method (synthesized):
1. Choose the decision to study. Example: "buying strategy-planning software or hiring a consultant for the strategy cycle."
2. Recruit recent decision makers. Include winners, losses and "no decision" cases.
3. Open each interview with the moment the work started. Then follow the story. Use Mom Test rules.
4. Code the answers into the 5 Rings. Use buyer quotes, not team opinions.
5. Stop at saturation, usually around 8 to 15 interviews per role.
6. Write one detailed persona for the champion. Add an objection list for the economic buyer, IT and finance.
7. Write user personas separately for daily users of the execution module.
- Anti-persona-theater checks (inference): every persona claim links to at least one interview quote; no stock photos, hobbies or invented names unless they help a team remember; personas are re-checked after each sales cycle.
- For a Turkish market, interviews should be held in Turkish. Quotes should be kept in the original language for messaging use.

### Gaps
- The Buyer Persona Manifesto PDF could not be read as text. Revella's exact ring definitions and her own interview count were not verified.
- No primary source quantified the harm of fictional personas.

## Product positioning methods: Dunford, Moore, Ries and Trout, category design; brand positioning: Keller, archetypes, naming

### Takeaway
Dunford's method is the current practical standard for B2B SaaS. Its order matters: competitive alternatives, then unique attributes, value, best-fit customers, and market category. Moore's template is a useful one-sentence summary of the result. Category design (Play Bigger) is a high-cost, high-risk option. Brand positioning (Keller) adds a frame of reference, points of parity and points of difference. Naming needs linguistic checks in both Turkish and English, plus domain and trademark checks.

### Cited Findings
**April Dunford ("Obviously Awesome", 2019; "Sales Pitch", 2023)**
- Definition: positioning defines how your product is a leader at delivering something a well-defined set of customers cares a lot about — [April Dunford, quickstart guide](https://www.aprildunford.com/post/a-quickstart-guide-to-positioning)
- Five components, in order: (1) competitive alternatives, meaning what customers would do if the product did not exist, including doing nothing; (2) differentiated capabilities; (3) value those capabilities create; (4) target customers who care most about that value; (5) market category, the context that makes the value obvious — [April Dunford, quickstart guide](https://www.aprildunford.com/post/a-quickstart-guide-to-positioning). The same guide is republished on [Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/positioning).
- Order matters because differentiation only exists relative to alternatives. Dunford says that starting from features and testing on prospects took her about two years; positioning that starts elsewhere can sound good internally but is not differentiated for customers — [April Dunford, quickstart guide](https://www.aprildunford.com/post/a-quickstart-guide-to-positioning)
- Competitors should enter positioning only once customers actually meet them in deals — [April Dunford, quickstart guide](https://www.aprildunford.com/post/a-quickstart-guide-to-positioning)
- Positioning is never "one and done" (2026 AMA recap) — [Business of Software, February 2026](https://businessofsoftware.org/2026/02/why-positioning-is-never-one-and-done-key-lessons-from-april-dunfords-bos-ama)
- "Sales Pitch" (2023) structure is not detailed here; see Gaps.

**Geoffrey Moore positioning statement (Crossing the Chasm, 1991, classic)**
- Template: "For [target customer] who [statement of need or opportunity], the [product name] is a [market category] that [statement of key benefit]. Unlike [primary competitive alternative], our product [statement of primary differentiation]." — [Umbrex (secondary)](https://umbrex.com/resources/frameworks/marketing-frameworks/positioning-statement-framework-for-target-who-need-our-brand-is/). Wording varies by edition and source.
- Warning: "Unlike traditional approaches" is a straw man; name the alternative buyers really consider — [Medium/h7w (secondary)](https://medium.com/h7w/product-positioning-edfe6679f6f4)

**Category design (Play Bigger, 2016)**
- Category design is the discipline of creating and developing a new market category and conditioning the market to demand your solution, crowning a "category king" — [Single Grain (secondary)](https://www.singlegrain.com/marketing-strategy/what-is-category-design-in-marketing-and-why-is-it-an-important-strategy/); [Stanford eCorner talk](https://stvp.stanford.edu/videos/how-to-be-a-category-king-entire-talk)
- Play Bigger claims category kings take 76% of the market cap in their category — via [Techstars worksheet (secondary)](https://toolkit.techstars.com/understand-category-design-worksheet); see also [Stanford eCorner clip](https://stvp.stanford.edu/clips/data-on-category-and-company-value). The figure comes from the authors' own analysis; treat with caution (survivorship bias).
- Authors: Al Ramadan, Dave Peterson, Christopher Lochhead and Kevin Maney — [Medium summary](https://medium.com/@mitchrencher/play-bigger-ed0674088517)

**Keller brand positioning (HBR 2002, classic)**
- Keller, Sternthal and Tybout, "Three Questions You Need to Ask About Your Brand," HBR September 2002: (1) Have we established a frame of reference? (2) Are we leveraging points of parity? (3) Are the points of difference compelling? — [HBR store](https://store.hbr.org/product/three-questions-you-need-to-ask-about-your-brand/R0209F); [Kellogg School](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/academics-research/research/detail/2002/three-questions-you-need-to-ask-about-your-brand/)
- The frame of reference signals the goal customers can expect to achieve with the brand; some points of parity are required to be seen as a legitimate option; a brand can neutralize a rival's advantage by matching it as a parity point; past differentiators become minimum requirements over time — [HBR store abstract](https://store.hbr.org/product/three-questions-you-need-to-ask-about-your-brand/R0209F)

**Naming for Turkish and English**
- A Turkish guide proposes a first filter: up to three syllables, few Turkish-only letters (ç, ş, ğ, ı, ö, ü), not a generic product category word; check meanings in customers' languages; a TÜRKPATENT preliminary search is not a legal opinion — [Adapte Dijital (Turkish agency)](https://adaptedijital.com/?p=73517)
- Linguistic screening should confirm names are pronounceable and free of negative associations in target languages; Catchword uses three native-speaker linguists per language — [Catchword](https://catchwordbranding.com/linguistics-research/)
- Check domain availability during naming, not after — [Spaceship](https://www.spaceship.com/blog/saas-product-name-guide/). One SaaS guide suggests a .com under 12 characters, no hyphens or numbers, and early trademark filing — [PayPro Global](https://payproglobal.com/how-to/name-your-startup-in-saas/)
- International naming needs linguistic, cultural and legal analysis at the same time — [Spellbrand](https://spellbrand.com/blog/brand-names-cross-borders)
- Catchword suggests a naming process of at least 6 to 8 weeks, excluding legal vetting — [Catchword](https://catchwordbranding.com/create-the-perfect-brand-name)

### Inferences
Step-by-step product positioning for this SaaS (synthesized from Dunford and Moore):
1. List the real alternatives. Examples: Excel and PowerPoint, strategy consultants, OKR tools, BI dashboards, ERP planning modules, or doing nothing.
2. List what only this product does. Examples: one flow from pre-strategy diagnosis to execution tracking, Turkish-language method content, or local regulation templates.
3. Translate each capability into value. Example: "the board sees in one screen whether the strategy is on track."
4. Name the customers who care most about that value. This becomes the best-fit customer and feeds the ICP.
5. Choose the market category. Test whether buyers already have a budget line for it.
6. Write the Moore sentence as a summary. Use it inside the team, not as website copy.
7. Test the positioning in 10 or more sales calls. Revise it after each cycle.
- Category choice is a budget question. "Strategy execution software" or "OKR software" may already have buyer budgets. A newly invented category would require educating the market, which is expensive for a small Turkish startup. A safer path is to enter an existing category with a clear sub-category angle. Dunford's book discusses this option; see Gaps.
- Brand positioning layer (Keller): frame of reference = strategy-planning software; points of parity = security, KVKK compliance, Turkish and English interface, integrations; points of difference = one end-to-end method from preparation to execution, local method expertise.
- Brand archetypes (Jung-based, such as "Sage" or "Ruler") are popular in agency work. No research-grade evidence for them was found. They are best used as a tone-of-voice aid after positioning is decided, not as the positioning itself.
- Naming criteria for a Turkish and English SaaS (inference from sources): easy to say and spell in both languages; avoids ç, ş, ğ, ı, ö, ü in the core name for URLs and international use; no negative or silly meaning in Turkish, English, Arabic or German if these markets are planned; .com or .com.tr available or acquirable; TÜRKPATENT and EUIPO/WIPO searches clean in software classes (Nice classes 9 and 42 are typical for SaaS — not verified in this session); not a generic word that cannot be registered.

## Can one product target multiple audiences? Segment-specific positioning, good-better-best packaging, land-and-expand

### Takeaway
Yes, but Dunford's view is that one product normally keeps one positioning. Segments get different messaging, case studies and campaigns. If segments need different alternatives, capabilities and value, that is a product strategy problem, not a positioning problem. Packaging (separate tiers or modules) is a pricing decision, separate from positioning.

### Cited Findings
- "Multiple target segments rarely need distinct positioning." Competitors may differ across segments, but differentiated capabilities and value usually do not — [April Dunford, multi-segment guide](https://aprildunford.substack.com/p/a-guide-to-positioning-with-multiple)
- If segments truly need different positioning, the product may need to be forked; the real question becomes whether to serve both markets at all — [April Dunford, multi-segment guide](https://aprildunford.substack.com/p/a-guide-to-positioning-with-multiple); [Business of Software AMA recap, 2026](https://businessofsoftware.org/2026/02/why-positioning-is-never-one-and-done-key-lessons-from-april-dunfords-bos-ama)
- Messaging can be tailored by segment: homepage copy stays broad, while campaigns, terminology and case studies are segment-specific; the core pitch stays the same and the proof step uses a customer from the prospect's segment — [April Dunford, multi-segment guide](https://aprildunford.substack.com/p/a-guide-to-positioning-with-multiple)
- Multi-product options: umbrella company positioning with cascading product positioning; a lead product plus add-ons sold to existing customers; a family or platform positioned together; or split positioning for very different buyers. Whether products can be bought separately is a pricing and packaging decision, not a positioning decision — [April Dunford, multi-segment guide](https://aprildunford.substack.com/p/a-guide-to-positioning-with-multiple)
- Contrary practitioner view: each segment needs slightly different positioning on the same core product — [library-d7m (secondary)](https://library-d7m.pages.dev/product-management/go-to-market/positioning-and-messaging). This is a minority view relative to Dunford.

### Inferences
- The three product stages (pre-strategy preparation, strategy formulation, strategy execution) fit Dunford's "lead product" pattern. One stage is sold first and the others expand the account. This is a land-and-expand path. Example: land with strategy formulation for the board, then expand to execution tracking for department heads.
- Good-better-best tiers could map to the three stages or to company size. This is a packaging decision and should be tested with willingness-to-pay interviews.
- If both SMEs and large holdings are targeted, the alternatives are likely different (SMEs: Excel or nothing; holdings: consultants and BI tools). Per Dunford, check whether differentiated value also differs. If it does, pick one segment first.

### Gaps
- No primary 2024 to 2026 source on good-better-best packaging (for example Madhavan Ramanujam's "Monetizing Innovation" or Simon-Kucher writing) was fetched.
- No primary land-and-expand benchmark data (net revenue retention by motion) was gathered.

## Common mistakes: targeting everyone, persona theater, positioning by features, choosing a category with no buyer budget

### Takeaway
The recurring mistakes are too-broad ICPs, personas built from assumptions, positioning that starts from features rather than alternatives, straw-man competitors, and categories that buyers have no budget for. Each mistake has a direct counter-method in the sources above.

### Cited Findings
- Too broad: "any company with sales teams" is not an ICP — [Userpilot](https://userpilot.com/blog/?p=354756). Vendor rule of thumb: an ICP that does not disqualify most inbound is too broad — [Prospeo (vendor)](https://prospeo.io/s/ideal-customer-criteria)
- Firmographics-only ICP is a mistake; add triggers, intent and pain — [Landbase (vendor)](https://www.landbase.com/blog/why-firmographics-alone-dont-define-icp); [Saas Hero](https://www.saashero.net/strategy/b2b-saas-target-market-personas/)
- Persona theater: personas for every stakeholder were "mostly unhelpful"; focus on the champion — [April Dunford](https://aprildunford.substack.com/p/a-guide-to-positioning-with-multiple). Personas should come from real buyers' words, not assumptions — [Buyer Persona Institute](https://www.buyerpersona.com/)
- Positioning by features: starting from features produced positioning that was not differentiated for customers — [April Dunford, quickstart guide](https://www.aprildunford.com/post/a-quickstart-guide-to-positioning)
- Straw-man alternatives ("unlike traditional approaches") are not recognized by buyers — [Medium/h7w (secondary)](https://medium.com/h7w/product-positioning-edfe6679f6f4)
- Pitching instead of learning in interviews produces compliments, not data — [Shortform on The Mom Test](https://www.shortform.com/blog/what-is-the-mom-test/)
- Ignoring the buying group: 74% of buyer teams show unhealthy conflict; consensus groups are 2.5 times more likely to report a high-quality deal — [Gartner, May 2025](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-05-07-gartner-sales-survey-finds-74-percent-of-b2b-buyer-teams-demonstrate-unhealthy-conflict-during-the-decision-process?authuser=0)
- Stale stats: many buying-group numbers in circulation are years old — [Demand Gen Report](https://www.demandgenreport.com/?p=54785)
- Stale positioning: positioning must be revisited as markets change — [Business of Software, 2026](https://businessofsoftware.org/2026/02/why-positioning-is-never-one-and-done-key-lessons-from-april-dunfords-bos-ama)
- Naming late: checking domains after choosing a name wastes weeks — [Spaceship](https://www.spaceship.com/blog/saas-product-name-guide/)

### Inferences
- Category with no buyer budget: a likely risk for this product is choosing a label such as "strategy intelligence platform" that no Turkish buyer has a budget line for. The safer test is to ask in interviews where the money for strategy work comes from today (consultant fees, training budget, IT budget).
- Interviewing only friendly contacts is a Mom Test failure. It is especially likely in Turkey's relationship-driven B2B market (inference, not sourced).
- Confusing ICP with persona leads to messaging aimed at companies rather than people, or the reverse.
- Naming the brand before the positioning is settled risks a name that fits the wrong category.

### Gaps
