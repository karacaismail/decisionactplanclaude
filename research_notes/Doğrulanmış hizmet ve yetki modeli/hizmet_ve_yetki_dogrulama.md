## 1. Verification table (every flagged item)

### Takeaway
Three findings change a security rule. (a) The MCP endpoint is ON by default since v16.53.0, and a migrate patch switches it ON for existing sites. (b) A list-condition hook that returns an empty string opens the whole table. (c) The Frappe docs name the hook argument `permission_type`, but the code passes `ptype`. Two findings change the service model: the 15-minute weekly check is not from the OKR case, and the Kapasite Geliştirme programme is an interest subsidy on bank loans, not a KOSGEB loan or grant. The "73% vs 29%" Prosci figure was found in Prosci's own 11th Edition summary and can be restored with a vendor label.

## 2. Frappe 16: deeper facts on hooks, sharing, auth, MCP, naming and workflow

### Takeaway
The code confirms the report's design. It adds four required controls: an explicit-`True` hook with the `ptype` argument, a list hook that returns a deny clause for non-members, an active MCP switch-off with a release gate, and an app-level self-review block.

### Cited Findings
- Hook stricter rule came from PR #24253 in January 2024, stated as: "has_permission hook need to explicitly return "True" (or truthy) value to allow user. They will be blocked otherwise." Source: [commit 5ef8577](https://github.com/frappe/frappe/commit/5ef8577cffe4f9ff8ca948f0320e88c58a042750).

- `get_list` and `get_all` moved to the new query builder backend in v16, and default ordering moved from `modified` to `creation` (same wiki page). Code: [`__init__.py` L1376-L1378](https://github.com/frappe/frappe/blob/49789a1711d4dde2be9c6888efe35651465f4b64/frappe/__init__.py#L1376-L1378).

## 3. Service evidence: do management interventions last?

### Takeaway
Two randomized trials, read in full, show consulting effects persist for five to nine years, but part of the practice gain fades. In the Indian trial the main cause was a new plant manager. In the Mexican trial the main channel was owners defining goals and a growth strategy with the consultant.

### Cited Findings
- India sample: 28 plants in 17 family-owned woven cotton firms near Mumbai, large by Indian standards. Revisit in January 2017 with the same consulting team. All 17 firms agreed. Source: [AEJ Applied 2020](https://www.aeaweb.org/doi/10.1257/app.20180369), Section I-II, p.199-205.
- Management score in treatment experimental plants: 0.25 before, 0.60 at end of 2011, 0.46 in 2017. "This drop of 0.14 points in the management score reverses 40 percent of the original 0.35 increase", about 6% annual depreciation. Control plants lost 66% of a smaller gain. Treatment effect 20.6 percentage points in 2011 and 19.7 in 2017. Source: AEJ Section II.A p.205-206, and [NBER w24249](https://www.nber.org/papers/w24249) Section II.B.
- Table 3 (share of practice-by-plant cells, 2011 to 2017), treatment experimental plants: dropped 16.7% in total, of which new manager 9.9, reduced director time 3.9, perceived negative benefit 2.9. All plants: dropped 12.4% (new manager 4.6, negative benefit 4.2, director time 3.6), added 4.1%. Source: AEJ Table 3 p.210.
- Version difference: in the published table, control experimental "negative perceived benefit" is 9.3 and total dropped 14.7. The NBER version shows 5.3 and 10.7. Source: AEJ Table 3 p.210 vs NBER Table 3 (PDF page 34).
- Within-firm spread: non-experimental plants of treated firms copied practices (17.5 points by 2010) and by 2017 matched the experimental plants. Spread across firms was rare. Source: NBER Sections I.B and II.B.
- Imputed labour productivity 35% higher after 8 years. Treated firms later bought more local consulting on their own. Source: AEJ p.200 and Section II.D. NBER Section II.D.
- Authors' conclusion: the "three-year life" view of change programmes is "decisively rejected" for these practices. Source: AEJ Section III p.217.
- Mexico: 432 SMEs in Puebla applied. 150 randomly offered subsidized consulting. 80 took it up. Programme ran March 2008 to February 2009, 4 hours a week per firm, cut from a planned 2 years to 1 year for funding reasons. Firms paid 10% (micro), 20% (small) or about 30% (medium) of cost. Full cost about USD 57 an hour, USD 11.856 per firm per year. Source: [JPE 2018](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/121090), Section II p.641-642.
- Mexico results: after one year TFP and return on assets rose by about one fifth of a standard deviation, profits by about one tenth, not robust to all specifications. Using social security data, employees rose 57% and the wage bill 72% over 2010-2014. Table 4 reports these as intent-to-treat estimates. Source: JPE p.637-638, Table 4 p.657.
- Mechanism: consultants said firms "lacked a clear vision and definition of goals". Consultants helped "define a growth strategy or business plan". Most improved practices: marketing, financial accounting, long-term business planning. Source: JPE abstract p.635 and p.638-639.
- Why control firms did not buy consulting (n=108): no funds though a good investment 46.3%, did not know the benefit 22.2%, had not considered it 18.5%, not worth the cost 5.6%. Source: JPE Table 8 p.668.

### Inferences
- The two triggers of decay in India map directly to Karar Atlası: the strategist or plan owner leaving, and the decision owner's time. A partner who calls when the decision owner changes targets the largest measured cause.
- The Mexican "lack of funds" answer (46.3%) supports using public support routes and a small, fixed-price pilot.
- Durable effects in both trials came after intensive help (India: months of on-site consulting. Mexico: 208 hours a year). An 8-week pilot is lighter. The quarterly partner meeting is the cheapest way to keep a human in the loop. This is an inference, not tested.

### Gaps
- Neither trial tested software plus facilitator against software alone. No such study was found in this round or the last.
- Karlan, Knight and Udry (2015) and Higuchi et al. (2016), cited for decay in smaller firms, were not read.

## 4. OKR case study, sponsor data and customer success benchmarks

### Takeaway
The OKR case is real and open access, but the 15-minute weekly check is a literature figure, not a case finding. Prosci's 73% vs 29% is genuine vendor data from the 2020 edition. Gainsight's ratios come from very large US software vendors.

### Cited Findings
- JBS case: author Christine Wowerath, University of Innsbruck. Journal of Business Strategy 47(1):52-70, published online 12 November 2025, CC BY 4.0. Single case in "a large European financial services and debt collection company", buy-now-pay-later unit. Pilot started Q1 2023, 14 product teams, three quarterly cycles (nine months). 11 interviews in mid-April 2023, "seven to eight weeks after the launch", average 40 minutes. Weekly check "integrated into existing weekly team meetings". Formal review at cycle end, then a retrospective. "certified OKR masters embedded in the teams and an OKR champion". An external consultant supported early phases. The 15-minute figure appears as general literature: "weekly meetings are held, typically lasting around 15 min". Source: [Emerald](https://www.emerald.com/jbs/article/47/1/52/1311605/Engaging-the-entire-organization-how-OKRs-enhance), methods, case and limitations sections.
- Prosci 11th Edition (2020): sponsor effectiveness vs met-or-exceeded objectives: 29%, 39%, 57%, 73%. Participants: 1.863. Source: [Prosci 11th Edition Executive Summary](https://www.prosci.com/hubfs/2.downloads/research-executive-summaries/11th%20Edition-Best-Practices-Executive-Summary-11-2020.pdf), slides 2 and 7.
- Prosci 12th Edition (2023): 2.668 respondents in 101 countries. Overall change management quality vs objectives met: 13%, 39%, 73%, 88% (poor to excellent). Source: [Prosci 12th Edition Executive Summary](https://prosci.com/hubfs/Website_English/2.downloads/webinars/EN-EUR%20Webinar/Best-Practices-in-Change-Management-12th-Edition-Executive-Summary.pdf), p.2 and p.7.
- Gainsight 2022: accounts per CSM 22 (>USD 100K ACV), 49 (USD 10K-100K), 144 (<USD 10K), from US enterprise customers above USD 100M ARR. ARR per CSM median USD 1.4M, top quartile USD 4.2M, from 17.034 CSMs. Source: [Gainsight 2022](https://www.gainsight.com/blog/gainsight-horizon-ai-labs-what-is-the-right-csm-to-customer-ratio/).
- Gainsight 2025 CS Index: Gainsight customers cover about 25% more accounts per CSM than non-customers in commercial and enterprise segments, nearly 70% more in SMB. Absolute values not in the text. Source: [Gainsight, 23 Ocak 2026](https://www.gainsight.com/blog/what-customer-success-teams-are-prioritizing-in-2026-and-what-the-data-shows-is-working/).
- StrategyFrame: about 100 Mittelstand customers. 40% basic SaaS only. "The other 60" add coaching with up to two workshops and at most 3 days per month over six months. Source: [The Innovator](https://theinnovator.news/?p=11868). Entry price 15.000 EUR a year with ten licences and one academy licence, vendor-supplied article dated 29 July 2025. Source: [it-daily](https://www.it-daily.net/it-management/projekt-personal/ki-plattform-ersetzt-mckinsey-co-fuer-einen-bruchteil-der-kosten).

### Inferences
- Support-hours estimate, reasoned: at 2 hours a month per customer and about 140 productive hours a month (assumption from the report), one person covers about 70 customers. Gainsight's 144 low-touch figure comes from large vendors with automation. So 70 is a reasonable early ceiling for a small team, not "half of the norm".
- StrategyFrame's 60% coaching attach rate implies a vendor-delivered or partner-delivered coaching line is normal in this segment. It is a founder claim.

### Gaps
- Gainsight 2025 absolute accounts-per-CSM values sit in a gated report. Routes tried: Gainsight blog pages, web search. Substitute: 2022 figures with sample caveat.
- Who delivers StrategyFrame coaching (staff or partners) is not stated in any source found.
